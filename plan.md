## 1. Vision & Goals

**Vision:** One reference set that can take a person from "I'm lost or the power's out tonight" to "I can feed, heal, shelter and provide for my family long-term," anywhere in the world.

**Goals**
- **Complete:** short-term survival, long-term self-reliance, and a world field guide in one set.
- **Usable under stress:** emergency "Red Pages" up front, clear icons, step-by-step how-tos, and an index you can find things in fast.
- **Global but local:** a world atlas plus about 55 regional field guides, so a reader can grab only the pages for their region.
- **Trustworthy:** every plant and animal entry lists its dangerous look-alikes, every herbal claim gets an evidence grade, and every food-safety process follows tested guidelines.
- **Works with no power:** a printed edition first, plus offline digital copies (PDF/EPUB and a searchable offline website).
- **Expandable:** species, rivers, crops and nutrients live in data files, so the book can grow without rewriting chapters.

**Who it's for (default assumption; confirm in §15):** capable adults, from beginners to intermediate. It assumes no prior skills but doesn't talk down to the reader.

## 2. Scope, Safety Principles & What's Out of Scope

### In scope
- How-tos for making, creating and building things (primitive → homestead → modern).
- Water, fire, shelter, navigation, signaling, disasters.
- Food: foraging, fishing, hunting/trapping (legal context), preservation, cooking, **recipes**.
- Nutrition: **vitamins, minerals**, calories, natural toxins in foods.
- First aid, hygiene, **herbal medicine, health tonics, common herbs**.
- **Gardening** and small livestock.
- **Materials**: woods, stones, clays, geological minerals, fibers, animal materials, basic chemicals, metals.
- **World atlas**: continents, regions, climate zones, biomes, oceans, seas, major rivers, lakes, mountains, deserts.
- **Plants and animals of every region**, rated edible, medicinal, toxic or deadly.
- **Blacksmithing & metalworking** in depth: from building a forge to finished tools, including making iron in a bloomery.
- **Psychoactive & intoxicating plants and fungi** (cannabis, peyote, datura, psilocybin mushrooms and others): identification, legal status, risks, signs of poisoning, first aid and harm-reduction resources.
- **Beekeeping, and raising animals, insects and other organisms** for food, fiber, feed and pollination: poultry, rabbits, goats, fish, worms, crickets, mealworms, black soldier flies, snails, silkworms and mushrooms.
- **Small machines & mechanisms** (water wheels, mills, pumps, presses), **pottery**, **historical ovens & cooking devices**, and **classic tools & gear** (axes, knives, water skins, packs, boats, sleds).
- **Hundreds of images & illustrations:** about 2,590 across the set (see §9).

### Editorial safety rules (non-negotiable)
1. **No plant or fungus is called edible unless its dangerous look-alikes are listed** alongside it.
2. **Features needed for identification get a picture.** Text alone isn't enough to identify a plant.
3. **Every medicinal claim carries an evidence grade** (§7) plus contraindications and drug interactions.
4. **Canning and curing follow tested guidelines only** (USDA/NCHFP-style). No guessing about botulism.
5. **First aid matches current wilderness-medicine guidelines.** The book recommends real training (WFA/WFR, CPR).
6. **Legal notes where they matter:** foraging on public land, hunting/trapping/fishing licenses, protected species, wells, radio licensing.
7. **Myths get called out.** Examples: drinking urine or seawater, sucking out venom, "animals eat it so it's safe," moss growing only on the north side.
8. **Mushrooms get extra-strict rules** and a "never eat based on this book alone" warning.
9. **Psychoactive plants and fungi are covered for recognition and safety only:** what they look like, their look-alikes, legal status, risks, signs of poisoning or overdose, and first aid. There are no dosing, preparation, extraction or combination instructions. Readers are pointed to harm-reduction organizations instead.

### Out of scope (deliberately excluded)
- Making explosives or incendiaries, including black powder and thermite. Neither is needed for smithing. Metal is joined by forge welding, which Part 3I covers in full.
- Making firearms or weapons beyond traditional hunting and fishing tools (bows, spears, slings, atlatls) and forged knives and axes. Basic firearm *safety and maintenance* is fine.
- Drug synthesis or extraction, and dosing or preparation instructions for any psychoactive substance.
- Anything aimed at harming people.

## 5. Regional Field Guide Framework

### How regions are defined
Regions follow **climate and biome** as much as political borders, because plants and animals don't stop at borders. Each region gets a code (e.g., `NA-07`) used in the species database.

### Where species entries live (hybrid design)
- **Full entries** go in the region where a species is most typical.
- **Every region gets a checklist** of all its relevant species with page references (built automatically from the database).
- **Species found worldwide** live once in Part 12.
- This keeps the book usable in the field (carry your region's pages) without printing the same entry 15 times.

## 7. Rating & Icon System

The symbols must work in **black-and-white printing** and for **color-blind readers**, so they use shape plus a letter, never color alone.

### Plants & fungi
| Symbol | Code | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| ● | **E** | Edible (stated parts, raw OK) |
| ◐ | **P** | Edible **only with preparation** (cooking, leaching, specific part or season) |
| ◇ | **M** | Medicinal / use with caution |
| ▲ | **T** | Toxic: causes illness |
| ✖ | **D** | **Deadly:** small amounts can kill |
| ⚠ | **L** | Has dangerous **look-alike(s)** |
| ☐ | **S** | Skin/contact hazard (e.g., poison ivy, wild parsnip, giant hogweed) |
| ✱ | **X** | Psychoactive / intoxicating (see Part 7D) |

### Animals
| Code | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **F** | Good food source |
| **V** | **Venomous** (injects toxin by bite or sting) |
| **P** | **Poisonous** (toxic to eat or touch) |
| **A** | Aggressive / dangerous to humans |
| **Dz** | Disease or parasite risk |
| **Pr** | Protected (legal restrictions) |

> The book teaches the difference: *venomous* means it bites or stings you; *poisonous* means it hurts you if you eat or touch it.

### Evidence grades (herbal & folk remedies)
| Grade | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **A** | Strong clinical evidence |
| **B** | Some clinical evidence (small or mixed studies) |
| **C** | Traditional use with plausible mechanism, little human research |
| **T** | Traditional use only / unproven |

## 10. Style Guide (Summary)

- **Reading level:** about 8th grade. Short sentences, active voice.
- **Units:** both systems, imperial first, metric in parentheses (or reverse it; see §15).
- **Names:** common name first, *scientific name* in italics on first mention in each entry.
- **Steps:** numbered, one action per step.
- **Callout boxes:**
  - ⚠ **Warning:** could injure or kill
  - ⛔ **Do not:** common deadly mistakes
  - 💡 **Tip:** practical shortcut
  - 🧭 **Myth vs. Fact**
- **Every entry ends with cross-references** ("See also: …").
- **No absolutes on safety** unless they're true ("never eat a mushroom you haven't confirmed with an expert" is a true absolute).
- **Respectful sourcing of Indigenous and traditional knowledge:** credit cultures where practices come from, and don't present sacred practices as how-tos.
- A full `style-guide.md` is written in Phase 1.

## 13. Legal, Licensing & Disclaimers

- **Write original text.** Facts can't be copyrighted, but other guides' wording, tables and layouts can. Use other books for facts, not for text.
- **Track every image's license** (§9). This matters most if you sell the book.
- **Disclaimer** in every volume: educational, not medical or legal advice; laws vary; take training; consult experts.
- **If selling:** have a lawyer review the disclaimer, check that the title isn't trademarked, and consider an LLC.
- **Legal notes** throughout for foraging, hunting, trapping, wells, water rights, radio licensing and protected species.

> I'm not a lawyer. This is a checklist of things to look into, not legal advice.

This guide is a field reference, not a novel. Use the menu or search to jump straight to what you need. In an emergency, go to the Red Pages first; everything else can wait.

Finding Things Fast

  1. Emergency? Press the red Emergency button at the top of every page. It opens the Red Pages: bleeding, CPR, cold, heat, bites, poisoning, water and signaling.
  2. Search works on every page. Type a plant, a skill or a problem ("hemlock", "boil water", "frostbite").
  3. The menu lists all 15 parts of the guide. A filled green dot means a chapter is written and fact-checked. A hollow dot means it is planned but not written yet.
  4. Each chapter has an "On this page" list so you can skip to the section you need.

Plant and Fungus Ratings

Every plant and fungus gets one or more ratings. Each one uses a shape and a letter as well as a color, so the ratings still work in black-and-white print and for color-blind readers.

BadgeMeaning
● EEdible. The stated parts can be eaten, at the stated time of year, once you are certain of the identification.
◐ PEdible only with preparation: cooking, leaching, drying, or only one part or one season.
◇ MMedicinal, use with caution. Check the evidence grade and the "Do not use if" box.
▲ TToxic. Can make you sick.
✖ DDeadly. Small amounts can kill.
⚠ LHas dangerous look-alikes. Read the comparison before you pick anything.
☐ SSkin hazard. Touching it can burn, blister or rash the skin.
✱ XPsychoactive or intoxicating. Covered for recognition and safety only.

Warning

A rating is never permission to eat a plant. You still need a certain identification using several features, and for anything with an ⚠ L badge, confirmation from an experienced person in the field.

Evidence Grades for Remedies

Herbal and folk remedies are graded by how much good evidence supports them.

GradeMeaning
AStrong evidence from clinical studies in people.
BSome clinical evidence: small or mixed studies.
CTraditional use with a plausible reason it could work, but little research in people.
TTraditional use only. Unproven.

About the Illustrations

Every illustration in this guide is an original line drawing made for it. There are no photos. Plant and mushroom drawings are schematic: they show the features to look for, not every detail of a real specimen. Never identify a plant from a drawing alone. Use several features, more than one guide, and an experienced person.

Units and Numbers

Measurements are given in US units first, with metric in parentheses: 1 gallon (3.8 L), 212°F (100°C). When sources disagree on a number, the guide says so and gives the more cautious figure.

Using the Guide Offline

  • Host it at home. The whole guide is a folder of plain web pages. Any simple web server (a home server, a NAS, or a Raspberry Pi) can serve it on your home network, and it keeps working there when the internet is down.
  • Print it. Pages print cleanly without the menu. A printed copy of the Red Pages belongs in every kit.
  • Search works offline because the search index ships with the site.

Tip

Phones and servers fail when you need them most. Print the Red Pages and the chapters for your own region, and keep them in your vehicle and your emergency kit.

Corrections

Facts change. Agencies update first-aid advice and laws. Each written page shows the date it was checked. If something looks wrong, trust current official guidance and your training over this guide, and flag the page for a fix.

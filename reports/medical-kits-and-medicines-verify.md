# Verify report: medical-kits-and-medicines (Part 7A, Medical Kits & Medicines)

Verifier date: November 16, 2026. File: /home/claude/survival-site/content/medical-kits-and-medicines.html

## Changes made
1. **Epinephrine storage (safety fix).** Removed "sources differ on whether auto-injectors need a fridge" (Baystate claim). The EpiPen maker's storage page says 68-77°F (20-25°C), in the carrier tube, protected from light, **do not refrigerate**, do not leave in a car or glove box. Baystate source removed. Source: epipen.com HCP safety and storage.
2. **Insulin storage.** Added the FDA figures: refrigerated about 36-46°F (2-8°C); vials and cartridges may be kept at 59-86°F (15-30°C) for a limited time; never use insulin that has been frozen. No dosing or switching info added. Source: FDA insulin storage page.
3. **Room temperature.** Figure 3 caption now gives the USP controlled room temperature: 68-77°F (20-25°C), with brief excursions to 59-86°F (15-30°C). This replaces "sources vary (some say 59°F)". Source: USP <659> page (USP has also proposed changing CRT to 15-25°C). Figure 3's "Most medicines are no longer safe if frozen" changed to "Do not let medicines freeze. Check the label" (the old line overclaimed).
4. **FDA position on expired drugs.** Added that the FDA advises against using expired medicines, and that expired antibiotics may fail. Added the FDA warning about damp bathroom cabinets. Source: FDA, "Don't Be Tempted to Use Expired Medicines."
5. **Expired epinephrine paragraph rewritten.** The FARE page and the "2006 DoD article" could not be re-opened, so they were replaced with Simons 2000 (51-102% of labeled content, lower blood levels in rabbits) and Cantrell 2017 (40 pens, 1-50 months past date, all at 80% or more, falling over time). Kept the conservative rule: carry in-date pens; use an expired, non-discolored pen only if nothing else is available; call 911. Added the maker's rule to replace pens with pink or brown liquid or particles. Sources: Pharmaceutical Journal (Cantrell); ALiEM (secondary summary of both); epipen.com.
6. **OTC warnings table, checked against MedlinePlus:**
   - Loperamide: taking more than the label says can cause dangerous heart rhythm problems; not for children under 2; ask a doctor first if there is fever, blood or mucus in the stool, or black stool.
   - Diphenhydramine: drowsiness, so no driving or alcohol; not for young children unless a doctor directs; generally avoided at 65 and older except for serious allergic reactions.
   - Loratadine/cetirizine: can still cause drowsiness. "Not a treatment for a severe reaction" became "for a severe reaction, use prescribed epinephrine and call 911" (MedlinePlus Anaphylaxis).
   - Hydrocortisone: keep out of eyes and mouth; do not bandage over it unless a doctor says to; stop if not better in 7 days or if signs of infection appear.
7. **Counterfeit tourniquets.** Added that fakes can look nearly identical, so you cannot rely on spotting one by eye (EMS1), and that the FDA has also warned about counterfeit CATs with weak windlasses (SecuringIndustry report).
8. **"Do not rely on past date" table.** Insulin and nitroglycerin rows now cite SingleCare (pharmacist-reviewed; secondary) and the FDA frozen-insulin rule.
9. **Figures.** Fig 2: "Windlass rod" and "Rod clip" labels overlapped, so Rod clip moved right with an angled leader; the Time tag label was moved off its arrow. Fig 4: "Yes" labels sat on the arrows and box edges, so they were moved. Fig 5: box 3 text ran past the border, so it was shortened to "Date each pouch". Fig 5 caption said "two-month rhythm" but the figure shows twice a year, so the caption was corrected.
10. **Sources list.** Removed FARE and Baystate. Added FDA (2), USP, EpiPen, MedlinePlus (loperamide, hydrocortisone, diphenhydramine, loratadine, naproxen, anaphylaxis), Pharmaceutical Journal, ALiEM, EMS1, SecuringIndustry, and SingleCare. Verified date updated to November 16, 2026.

## Claims verified
- No doses anywhere. The only numbers are times or labels: 7 days for hydrocortisone, children under 2, 20 weeks of pregnancy, 60 or older, and 65 or older. The "use as directed on the label" callout is present.
- Acetaminophen (MedlinePlus): liver damage that can need a transplant or cause death; hidden in combination products; three or more drinks a day; overdose signs; get help even with no symptoms.
- Ibuprofen and naproxen (MedlinePlus, FDA Drug Facts label): ulcers and bleeding, age 60 or older on the label, heart attack and stroke, 20 weeks of pregnancy, dehydrated children, do not combine with other NSAIDs.
- Reye syndrome and aspirin/salicylates: kept as written (writer's MedlinePlus source).
- SLEP (PubMed 16721796): 122 products, 3,005 lots, 88% extended at least 1 year, average 66 months, highly variable, needs lot testing, depends on proper storage.
- CoTCCC list (15 Dec 2021): the tourniquet and gauze names match the PDF. The list also includes CAT Gen 6 "until replaced," X-Stat, and iTClamp, which the chapter correctly does not call gauze. The 2019 lists match too. I found no newer official list; Deployed Medicine is login-only. The chapter already tells readers to check the current list.
- Fridge 36-46°F (FDA); USP controlled room temperature; Poison Help 1-800-222-1222 (MedlinePlus); do not flush, use a take-back program (MedlinePlus).

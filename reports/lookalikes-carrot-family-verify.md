Medscape also says the same thing and stays in the list. Added "snorkel" to the advice. → USDA ARS Poison Hemlock page; USU Extension Range Plants.
3. **Wild carrot bracts.** Changed "branched (forked)" to bracts "split into thin, thread-like branches". Sources: Ontario says "branched"; Illinois Wildflowers says pinnatifid with linear lobes; UC IPM says finely dissected into thread-like segments. **Poison hemlock bracts** were added: short and undivided (Illinois Wildflowers; one botanical source only, so attributed). **Figure 3 redrawn:** the wild carrot bracts now have pinnate side lobes, and the hemlock now has short unbranched bracts with a label. 4. **Water hemlock root liquid color.** "Yellowish" was presented as settled, but sources differ. The text now says yellowish turning reddish brown (TAMU), brown or straw-colored (USDA ARS), or yellow-orange (Helsenorge). The Figure 6 label now reads "Yellowish to brownish poisonous liquid". "Oily" stays out because no source supports it.
5. **"Most toxic plant" callout.** Changed from a `myth` box with an "Attribution:" lead-in to a `warn` box. It now opens with "one of the most toxic plants in North America". 6. **CDC case wording.** I removed "He survived because he got care before the seizures started", because the report does not draw that causal link. It now says he was already under hospital care when seizures and delirium began, and he survived. → CDC MMWR 1992.
7. **Poison hemlock onset.** Now attributed: GI symptoms as soon as 20 minutes (Maryland Poison Center); Arkansas says symptoms usually appear within minutes. Death in under 3 h untreated in severe cases (INCHEM) was kept.
8. **Poison hemlock skin contact.** The text called handling "usually not hazardous". It now adds that Arkansas reports skin and breathing reactions from touching or smelling the plant, and advises gloves.

## Claims verified (brief)
- CDC MMWR 1992 (Maine): 23-year-old ate 3 bites, vomited and convulsed within 30 minutes, died about 3 h after eating. The 39-year-old ate 1 bite and seized about 2 h later. Mild toxicity appears in 15–90 min. No antidote. Water hemlock causes most deaths from misidentified plants and looks like parsnips. Early vomiting may be somewhat protective.
- USDA ARS water hemlock: "most violently toxic plant that grows in North America". Side veins lead to the notches, not the tips. The rootstalk has chambers holding brown or straw-colored liquid. Underground parts are most toxic. The toxin has a carrot-like odor.
- TAMU: veins to the notches, not found in other Apiaceae. Chambered rootstock with yellowish liquid that turns reddish brown. 2–6 ft. Blooms June–Oct. Wet habitats. Leaves once to three times pinnate, leaflets 1–5 in. CT CAES: veins end in the sinus, base chambered, multiple fleshy taproots, smooth hollow purple-striped stems.
- INCHEM Conium: about 3 mg coniine causes symptoms. Lethal dose may be 6–8 fresh leaves. Death in under 3 h untreated in severe cases. Ascending paralysis, death by respiratory failure. "Mousy" odor. Maryland Poison Center: GI effects as soon as 20 min, toxic dose impossible to predict, no antidote, supportive care including benzodiazepines and ventilation.
- Cornell: poison hemlock 2–10 ft, hairless with purple blotches, dried material still toxic, poisonings involve children playing with stems or adults misidentifying. MSU: 6–10 ft, biennial rosette, sap plus UV causes blisters, blooms June–Aug.
- Wild carrot: UMN (1–4 ft, hairy hollow stems, dark center flower, bird's nest, carrot-smelling woody root, May–Oct, may cause phytophotodermatitis). Iowa State (hairs on stem and petiole important; linear leaves vs triangular in hemlock). Ontario (branched bracts; root fibrous). Cornell wild carrot (1–4 ft, hairy stems).
- Skin: UMN Pesky Plants (cover at least 48 h, wash with soap and water, works on overcast days). Poison.org (reaction within 24 h, peaks about 3 days, avoid sun 1–2 days, rinse eyes at least 15 min, 1-800-222-1222 free and 24 h). NY DOH (blisters within 48 h, sunscreen for months, wash clothes separately in warm water, steroid cream, eye blindness risk). UMaine (eye and face protection, wash and keep out of sun).
- Giant hogweed: UMN (15–20 ft, 1–4 in stalks, 5 ft leaves, 2.5 ft umbels, blindness, report it). Maine DACF (stems with rigid hairs). Poison.org (12–15 ft). Cow parsnip: NC State (4–15 ft, 3 leaflets, 6–8 in umbels, June–July, low toxicity, furanocoumarins). Maine (5–8 ft, up to 1 ft umbels, blooms weeks before hogweed).
- Fool's parsley: Helsenorge (15–60 cm, hanging involucels, no modern cases, limited data, fields, road verges and wasteland). Massey (up to 120 cm, aethusin, mistaken for parsley, bearded bracts).
- Illinois Extension 2026: wild carrot dense and flat vs looser rounded hemlock; foragers poisoned; never trust apps or AI.
- Poison Help 1-800-222-1222 is confirmed (Poison.org, Arkansas).

## Still uncertain / notes
- Poison hemlock bract description (short, undivided) has only one botanical source (Illinois Wildflowers). The web-search budget ran out before I could find a second, so it is attributed and called a non-safe test.
- Heights and onset times differ between sources. The chapter shows the ranges with attribution and gives the conservative (earlier onset, longer sun avoidance) advice.
- The "veins to tips in most other toothed leaves" line is general botany, implied by TAMU's statement rather than stated directly.
- Fool's parsley toxicity is disputed (old severe reports vs no modern cases). It is kept as toxic and attributed.
- Note for another chapter: foraging-principles.html (line ~538) says "The CDC calls water hemlock the most toxic native plant in North America". The 1992 CDC MMWR I opened does not say that; the "most violently toxic" wording is USDA ARS. Its verifier should check this.

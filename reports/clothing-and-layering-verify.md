# Verification report: clothing-and-layering (Part 1 Ch 4 "Clothing & Layering")

## Changes made
1. Head heat-loss myth callout rewritten. It no longer presents the Army 40-45% figure as a competing fact. It now says the figure traces to a military test where only the head was bare, and that with the whole body exposed the head gives off no more than about 10%. Source: Indiana University School of Medicine release on the 2008 BMJ "medical myths" review (secondary, flagged). It still notes the CDC line that hats cut heat loss from the head. Consistent with content/shelter.html (exact percentages treated as myth; cover head and neck, but a hat is not enough).
2. COLD acronym. The prose now gives the full military wording: keep clothing Clean, avoid Overheating, wear it Loose and in Layers, keep it Dry. The Figure 3 tile now reads "Loose Layers" and the caption was updated to match. Checked against FM 21-76 (cold-weather chapter) and the Defense Health Agency Cold Injury Prevention page; both give the same four letters.
3. Fabric claims. Removed the Gizmodo source and its claims ("holds many times its own weight", "wool repels liquid water", "polyester absorbs essentially none"). Replaced them with a peer-reviewed lab study of wet socks (Mansoor, Hes and Bajzik, Autex Research Journal 2021). Its findings: every fiber lost about half or more of its insulation when soaked; cotton lost the most and still held about 21% moisture after 8 h; polyester, nylon and polypropylene recovered their dry insulation within 6 h; wool kept the most insulation while wet. The text says this is one small study of socks. Updated the table cells for wool and polyester/nylon to match. Down vs synthetic was re-checked against REI (flagged secondary) and is unchanged.
4. Figure 4 dot chart re-rated. Polyester base, fleece and synthetic fill dropped from 3 to 2 dots for "insulates when wet", since no fiber is as warm wet as dry. Updated the caption.
5. Wind-chill example. Replaced the Army example (15 kt at -10°C = -23°C, an old pre-2001 table) with a value from the NWS chart: 0°F (-18°C) with a 15 mph (24 km/h) wind = -19°F (-28°C). The text notes that the Army manual's older table gives colder numbers. weather.gov pages were blocked, so the NWS chart was read from a copy hosted by Texas Tech HSC. I checked the value against the NWS formula: about -19°F.
6. UPF / color. Skin Cancer Foundation claims verified unchanged: UPF 50 lets about 2% through; UPF 30-49 is very good and 50+ is excellent; dark or bright colors block more; a white T-shirt is about UPF 7 dry and 3 wet; brim at least 3 in; loose straw lets UV through; loose fit is better. Reworded "sources disagree" as a trade-off with attribution: pale colors feel cooler per REI/WTA.
7. Vapor barrier / plastic sock liners. Added the DHA advice to dry vapor-barrier boots inside at least daily. Added "remove, dry feet, change the thin sock at least daily". Added a trench-foot warning with a cross-reference to Part 7 › Environmental Emergencies. Verified the Outside VBL claims (secondary).
8. Sock drying wording now matches the Army manual: unfolded, near the body, or hung on the pack.
9. Figure 7: the "Dry stuffing" label overlapped the ellipse. Moved the drawing right and the label up. Re-rendered and checked.
10. Sources: removed Gizmodo. Added the Autex study, the NWS wind chill chart (TTUHSC copy), and the IU School of Medicine release.

## Claims verified
- FM 21-76: COLD wording; the 40-45% head figure; wind chill example (old table); drying socks and boots (between bag shell and liner); desert night about 10°C and daytime 55°C (the manual elsewhere also says 60°C); keep shirt on; cover head; mineral-water rash; wool for night; shake out boots.
- DHA: COLD; remove layers as work or temperature rises; wait to remove layers after exertion in extreme cold; avoid cotton; avoid tight socks/boots; change damp socks; paper towels in leather boots; dry VB boots daily.
- CDC/NIOSH cold stress: several loose layers; hat; waterproof insulated boots; avoid cold metal.
- REI down vs synthetic; Skin Cancer Foundation UPF.
- Trench-foot prevention is consistent with content/environmental-emergencies.html: change socks, keep feet dry, loosen boots, move toes.

- Unchanged from the writer: the tropical clothing advice, the blanket mittens, the bread-bag liners and the repair stitches are unsourced and flagged in the text. The glove advice "both natural or both synthetic" rests on REI only (low stakes).

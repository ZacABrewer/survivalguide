## Unverified or disagreeing
- Head heat loss: Army manual says 40 to 45 percent; the CDC says only "a lot"; Part 2 › Shelter already treats exact percents as a myth. Chapter gives no firm number.
- Dark vs light color: Skin Cancer Foundation says dark/bright blocks more UV; REI/WTA say light reflects heat. Chapter states the trade-off and says sources disagree.
- Cotton in hot dry climates: REI and WTA say it can work; the Army manual says to keep clothes on to hold sweat. Not a conflict, but all from outdoor/Army sources, not a physiology paper.
- Cotton, wool, nylon, polyester water-absorption comparison comes from a secondary source (Gizmodo). The fabric dot chart (Figure 4) is my qualitative synthesis of REI and that source, labeled as such.
- Polypropylene: no source found comparing it directly; the chapter says "polyester and similar synthetics" rather than naming it.
- Tropical dressing: no strong source. The paragraph is labeled reasonable practice (quick-dry synthetics, long sleeves for insects).
- Mittens from a blanket: no source found. Steps are marked "a basic craft method, not a tested design."
- Plastic/trash-bag vapor barrier for feet: Outside says VBLs suit oversocks and bag liners and warns against use as clothing. The bread-bag/trash-bag sock technique is not directly sourced; labeled last resort with cautions.
- Leather boot drying: Army says slow drying between sleeping bag shell and liner; DHA says stuff with paper towels; NRA Family says by a fire. Chapter uses the slower, more conservative advice ("do not rush it with strong heat").
- Trench-foot care/treatment left to Part 7 › Environmental Emergencies; only prevention is repeated here.
- Wind chill example (15 knots at -10°C feels like -23°C) is from the Army manual via summarizer; not cross-checked against NWS wind chill tables.
- Sewing stitches (whipstitch, patch) are general craft knowledge; no source described them.

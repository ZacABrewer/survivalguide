# Verification report: Part 7 > Bones, Joints, Head & Spine (bones-joints-head-spine)

Verifier date: 2026-11-16. WebSearch used: 7 of 10.

## Changes made
1. **Spine, 2024 WMS guideline.** The full SAGE text and Europe PMC would not load. I read the WMS's own "2024 Spine Summary" (wms.org), the PubMed abstract (PMID 38379496), and a WMS magazine article saying the 2019 version was revised in 2024. The 2024 summary repeats the 2019 points: SMR rather than immobilization; no required role for rigid collars; soft collar optional; vacuum splint better than a rigid collar; no rigid collar in ankylosing spondylitis; vacuum mattress over backboard, with backboards for short moves only; no immobilization for isolated penetrating trauma; self-extrication for alert, reliable patients; light to moderate traction back to neutral; lateral position for airway problems; NEXUS/Canadian C-spine for trained users; lift-and-slide with trap squeeze preferred over log roll. New in 2024: do not say "clearing the spine"; jaw thrust over head tilt/chin lift; a decision chart is added; a person with neuro deficits gets soft passive SMR, not total immobilization.
   - What I changed in the chapter: removed "I could only open the abstract"; rewrote the SMR list to match the 2024 summary; removed "spine clearance" wording; added the jaw-thrust, neuro-deficit and remove-devices-promptly points (the last from the WMS magazine).
   - I renamed the log-roll section "Lift-and-slide first, log roll if you must." I added a short lift-and-slide description (BEAM / "many hands"; ASRC presentation). I added a warning that the log roll can worsen pelvic bleeding (ASRC presentation). The trap squeeze is named only, with no steps.
2. **Log-roll steps** are now sourced to StatPearls (EMS Long Spine Board Immobilization). They use 4 people at minimum and 5 if possible. The patient's arms cross over the chest, the head holder calls the count ("on three"), and the 5th person slides the pad in. Figure 6 now says "Chest" instead of "Shoulder", and the caption gives the lift-and-slide preference and the pelvis caveat.
3. **Dislocations** are now "trained responders only", with no step-by-step reduction.
   - Shoulder: removed the method descriptions (the gravity/hanging-weight position and self-traction) and the study sizes. Added a bold statement that shoulder reduction is for trained responders only.
   - Kneecap: removed the reduction technique and the "under 30 seconds" claim. Facts are now sourced to Merck: the kneecap sits out of place to the outside, the knee cannot be straightened, and the kneecap may slide back on its own but still needs checking for chip fractures.
4. **Traction splints.**
   - Contraindications are now backed by two sources: Crisis Medicine and the Journal of Paramedic Practice (2019). Both list pelvis and kneecap/shin/ankle fractures. Only Crisis Medicine adds partial amputation.
   - Added harms from Paramedic Practice: splints often misapplied, case reports of peroneal nerve palsy, and longer scene time.
   - Corrected the "2023 systematic review" to Philipsen et al., *Injury* 2022, as cited by Crisis Medicine. Stated that these are secondary summaries.
   - The "most lay rescuers should not build a traction splint" advice stays.
5. **Splinting and sling.** Checked against the FM 4-25.11 full text (Dec 2002, irp.fas.org). It confirmed: at least 4 ties (2 above, 2 below); knots on the splint away from the casualty; a fingertip should fit under the tie; no wire or cord; splint in the position found; do not push bone back. I updated the tie step to match. I removed the "about 10 degrees" sling angle, because FM 4-25.11 says only "slightly higher than the elbow" with the fingers visible.
6. **SAM splint shaping (gap filled).** Added a short sourced subsection. Per SAM Medical's product page and instructions (SP-IS-EN-1): curves give the splint its strength; the C, reverse-C and T curves are shown, with the T strongest; it can be cut with scissors, and cut edges must be taped or folded; secure it with a wrap or tape.
7. **Head injury.**
   - Reworded the CDC danger sign to match the source: loss of consciousness with increasing drowsiness, or unable to wake or stay awake.
   - Added MedlinePlus emergency signs (severe headache or stiff neck, can't move a limb, vomiting more than once, any loss of consciousness).
   - Added skull-base fracture signs from the Merck Professional TBI page (CSF or blood from the nose or ears, Battle sign, raccoon eyes). Added MedlinePlus's advice not to press on the wound or remove debris.
   - Added Merck's statement that even people discharged from hospital need an observer for 24 hours.
   - **Night checks (gap):** no authoritative source gives a waking schedule; NICE and NHS pages were blocked. The new step tells the reader to sleep next to the person and to rouse them if worried or if they were getting worse, because "unable to wake" is a CDC danger sign. It still defers the schedule to the course or medical direction.
   - Fixed a misquote: the CDC does not say "within 24 to 48 hours". It says the child needs to be seen.
   - Added the CDC's lighter rest guidance (children): no bed all day, and light activity if it does not worsen symptoms. Kept the 2014 wilderness review's step-up plan and labeled its date.
   - Updated the warning callout: Merck names acetaminophen as the preferred concussion pain reliever (use as directed on the label); MedlinePlus says no alcohol or street drugs for 48 hours; the CDC says ask a doctor or nurse before giving a child sleep medicine.
   - Added a short "Do not" callout from MedlinePlus: do not shake a dazed person; do not remove a helmet, with an airway exception.
8. **Litters (gap filled).** Replaced the unsourced construction text with the FM 4-25.11 / IS0877 method.
   - Lay one pole along the middle, fold one half over it, lay the second pole on the middle of the doubled part, then fold the free edges over the second pole and back toward the first.
   - Also added: the jacket/shirt litter, the no-pole rolled-blanket carry, materials, two or four bearers, keep level, and replace with a real litter soon.
   - **Figure 8 was redrawn.** The old drawing showed blanket edges hanging outside the poles, which did not match the Army method. It now shows three end-view fold steps plus a top view.

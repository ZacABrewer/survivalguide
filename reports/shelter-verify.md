## Claims verified (brief)

- **Hypothermia:** can occur at 30-60°F with wind or rain; early signs are slurred speech, stumbling and impaired judgment; avoid alcohol, which increases heat loss (NPS Wrangell-St. Elias).
- **WMS 2019 / AAFP:**
  - a large amount of heat is lost to the ground
  - windproof and vapor layers cut convective and evaporative loss
  - significant heat loss from the head and neck
  - burns from lukewarm hot-water bottles placed on the skin; check skin every 20-30 min
  - heat goes on the armpits, chest and back
  - body-to-body warming is no faster than shivering
  - keep the patient horizontal and handle gently
- **Site selection:**
  - fall zone 1.5x height on flat ground, larger on slopes; no hammocks on dead trees; dead trees fall even in calm weather (Scouting America)
  - flash floods: most deaths at night, dry beds rise in minutes, don't camp on low ground by streams (NWS)
  - cold-air pooling (Mount Washington Observatory)
  - low ground is colder and has more insects (FM 21-76)
- **Shelter builds:** debris hut 1 m of debris and 30 cm of bedding, ridgepole at waist height; lean-to steps; swamp bed; snow trench; snow cave (3 m drift, 30 cm walls, platform above the entrance, vent); "never sleep directly on the ground"; mineral-water rash (all FM 21-76).
- **Quinzhee:** snowball test, 1 ft sticks, light through the wall means too thin, downwind entrance (AMC); lowest-side entrance, scoop don't pry, bottom walls thicker, LED lights only (NOLS).
- **Grand Canyon heat advice:** no hiking 10 a.m.-4 p.m.; drink to thirst; salty snacks; soak shirt, bandana and hat; heat-illness signs (GRCA pages and PDF).
- **Tarps:** A-frame at 45° with a 4-6 in gap, lean-to, plow point, diamond and tarp tent (retailer pages; low stakes, kept).

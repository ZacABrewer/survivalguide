## Changes made
1. **Spine caution (Before You Move Anyone).** Rewritten to agree with Part 7 › Bones, Joints, Head & Spine and the WMS 2024 guideline: the goal is spinal motion restriction, not rigid immobilization; no rigid collars; backboard only for short moves; neck in position of comfort; **lift-and-slide preferred over log roll**; log roll only if there are too few hands; avoid the log roll if the pelvis may be broken. Red Cross "keep still, support head, turn face-down person face-up as a unit" kept. Source: WMS 2024 Spine Summary (wms.org); Red Cross WRFA pocket guide.
2. **Blanket drag step 2 and litter-building step 5.** Changed "log roll or lift" to lift-and-slide first, log roll only if short of hands. Same source and reason.
3. **Fireman's carry.** Added the FM 4-25.11 caution to keep the head from snapping back during the lift (neck injury risk), noted that it loads the patient's chest and belly head-down, and said never to use it for a suspected neck or spine injury. Source: FM 4-25.11 Appendix B (opened directly at irp.fas.org).
4. **Litter team size.** JEMS flagged as a trade journal. Added JEMS figure of about 1 mph with 15 to 20 people. Clarified that the Army's "two or four bearers" is for short military moves, and gave a conservative plan: 6 to 8 on the litter plus at least two relief teams (consistent with JEMS 18+). Sources: JEMS (both articles), FM 4-25.11.
5. **Rotation method.** The old text ("front bearers end up at the back") had no source and contradicted the one source that describes it. Replaced with the ASRC method: relief walks ahead, lets the litter pass, joins at the rear, everyone shifts forward, the front pair peels off, crosses sides and rejoins the relief line; the litter keeps moving; bearers alternate arms. Figure 7 label updated to match. Source: ASRC Essentials for SAR.
6. **Feet-first vs head-first.** Sources differ (Army: feet first on level ground; ASRC: usually head first). Text and Figure 7 caption now say so and tell readers to follow the team leader. Uphill/leg-fracture rules kept (medtrng Army text).
7. **"Head end calls commands" (Figure 7) / "head-end bearer watches the patient".** Unsourced as stated; ASRC puts the litter captain at front-left. Changed to "assign one person to watch the patient; the head end is easiest" and the figure now says "One leader calls every lift, stop, and swap."
8. **Landing zone size.** Added Texas DSHS (state EMS) and Cleveland Clinic: at least 100 x 100 ft with no overhead obstructions. NOLS 60 ft kept as the differing figure. Conservative rule stated: 100 x 100 ft minimum, more at night or with trees on the approach.
9. **LZ slope.** Sources: Texas DSHS 3 degrees or less, Air Methods no more than 5, Beacon under 10. Now says aim for 3 degrees or less. Added cut brush flat at ground level (ASRC).
10. **Keep-out distance.** Conservative rule made explicit: 200 ft (Beacon); only the ground guide stands closer (Air Methods: 25 to 50 ft outside the zone).
11. **Added "no road flares"** for marking an LZ (Texas DSHS).
12. Sources list: added FM 4-25.11, WMS 2024 Spine Summary, Texas DSHS AirMed LZ Training, Cleveland Clinic LZ guide, ASRC Essentials for SAR. Verified date set to November 16, 2026.

## Claims verified
- NOLS (Satz 2006): evacuation spectrum, "when in doubt, get the patient out", fall over 15 ft and any trauma with loss of consciousness as evacuation criteria, simple carries not acceptable when c-spine protection is needed, litter carries costly and dangerous, a few minutes' delay unlikely to change outcome, one communicator, waiting too long can force a litter carry, wet snow on scree example. All match.
- Red Cross WRFA: stay-or-go factors, go-fast and go-slow lists (table entries match), info for rescuers, spinal guidance.
- JEMS: fast-evacuation criteria, 6/8 bearers, 18+ total, rotate every 400 to 500 yd or 10 to 15 min, rigid stretcher for technical rescues, air vs ground and meeting the aircraft.
- FM 4-25.11 Appendix B: pack-strap 50 to 300 m, palms-down; four-hand seat conscious only, 50 to 300 m, head or foot injuries; two-hand seat for short distances and litter placement with arms under thighs and back (matches the chapter's arm placement); two-man arms carry safest for back injury in an extreme emergency; neck drag not with a broken arm; no manual carries for suspected back or neck injury unless an immediate life threat; improvised litters are temporary.
- medtrng (Army-derived): two-hand seat up to 50 m; two extra helpers keep head and legs aligned on the two-man arms carry; straps chest and below knees, more for rough or steep ground; feet-first and slope rules; STEADY command; rear bearers watch front bearers.
- FAA AC 91-32B: approach from side or front in pilot's view, crouch, downhill side on slopes, never from the rear, long items horizontal at or below waist, loose items, hand signals (stop, land, hover, all clear), signalman back to wind.
- Air Methods: radio phrases "Go around" and "Stop, Stop, Stop", clock positions, one radio person, 150 ft crowd distance, leave the way you came.
- NOLS helicopter handbook: 60 ft diameter, anchored bright markers, approach only on pilot signal, never behind, not from uphill.
- Figures: all 8 rendered with qa_figures.py and checked. No overlaps or out-of-bounds labels. Figure 8 geometry is consistent (wind from the top, nose into the wind, guide upwind with back to the wind, tail-rotor danger zone at 6 o'clock).
- Format: parses cleanly; no hard-coded colors, style, script or h1; all ids slug-prefixed or section ids; sources and verified paragraph present.

## Changes made (reason; source)
1. Frostbite: 98.6-102.2°F (37-39°C) bath kept, now noted as unchanged in the 2024 WMS update (AAFP blog on the 2024 update; MSD Manual, Aug 2026 revision, which uses WMS 2024 as its main reference). The walking line now says avoid walking on a frozen foot, but walk on it still frozen rather than thaw it if walking out is the only option (AAFP 2020; MSD). Added aloe under bulky dressings and raising the limb above the heart (AAFP 2020). Fixed the broken "rub ... skin, ." bullet.
2. Heat stroke: the stop point stays at about 102°F (39°C) rectal (KSI; J Athl Train 2021 roundtable says 38.9°C). Added how long to cool without a thermometer: about 10-15 min (Korey Stringer Institute) or about 15 min / until no longer confused (Red Cross). Added the expected rate, about 1°F every 3 min in stirred cold water (KSI), plus a step to take the person out, dry them, watch, and re-cool if signs come back. Without these, the old text ("until thinking clears", no time limit) could overcool. "Cool first, transport second" is now credited to KSI, and the writer's "I did not find" line was removed. The 30-minute goal is now worded as cooling within about 30 min of collapse (roundtable). "Then arrange rescue" now says someone else calls while you cool. "Half as fast" for misting/fanning was softened to "much more slowly".
   - **Red Pages reconciliation:** the card's "about 15 minutes, or until they are no longer confused" matches the Red Cross page word for word and sits inside the KSI 10-15 min range. It is not wrong, so I left it alone. Both are no-thermometer rules. With a rectal thermometer, the target is about 102°F (39°C). The J Athl Train roundtable advises against fixed times where temperature can be measured.
3. Lightning: rewrote the "lightning position" section. The NWS stopped recommending the crouch in 2008 because it does not meaningfully protect you (NWS lightning-crouch page). WMS treats it as a last resort at most. Advice is now to keep moving toward shelter and to plan ahead. Distance is now "10 miles or more" and the wait "at least 30 minutes" (CDC). Added: do not lie flat, avoid cliffs and power lines, keep distance from any tree in woods (CDC; NWS). Figure 8's right panel was redrawn and relabeled "NWS: not recommended since 2008", with the caption and title updated.
4. Altitude: 500 m/day sleeping gain above 3,000 m and a rest day every 3-4 days are confirmed (MSD Manual citing WMS 2024; CDC Yellow Book citing WMS). Added "do not ascend to sleep while symptomatic" (CDC). The unverified "HAPE at least 3,300 ft" descent claim was replaced with: CDC says at least 300 m often brings quick relief for AMS, MSD gives 500-1,000 m for severe AMS, and HACE/HAPE need prompt descent as far as practical.
5. Hypothermia: Swiss/WMS staging and temperatures in Figure 1 match the MSD table (revised Swiss AVPU). The single shock below 30°C is confirmed (MSD). Removed the unsupported "afterdrop up to 5-6°C" figure and the "several degrees an hour" shivering-rewarm claim (softened). Wet-clothing text unchanged: sources still differ (Red Cross and MSD say remove; WMS says wait until sheltered), and the chapter keeps the cautious shelter-first rule.
6. Heat cramps: the treatment is now sourced. Stop and rest somewhere cool; sip water plus a sports drink, salty snack, or lightly salted drink (MedlinePlus: 1 tsp salt per quart); stretch or firmly massage; no salt tablets; get help if cramps last more than 1 h or the person has heart problems or is on a low-salt diet (CDC/NIOSH; MedlinePlus).

## Could not verify / sources disagree / limits
- **Blocked page:** Dartmouth-Hitchcock DHART landing zone page was refused by the permission layer; not retried and not cited. The govinfo "Field Care and Transportation of the Injured" text was opened but truncated before the litter chapters, so it is not cited.
- **Direct US Army manual not opened.** Carries, drags, poncho/jacket litters and litter handling come from Army-derived secondary sites (armystudyguide, medtrng), plus First Voice and CERT-LA. They agree with each other, but treat as secondary.
- **Landing zone size:** NOLS handbook says about 60 ft across; Beacon says at least 100 x 100 ft. Air Methods gave no dimensions. Chapter says use the larger. The 100 x 100 figure rests on one opened air-medical source (Beacon); the signaling chapter cites others I did not open.
- **LZ slope:** Beacon under 10 degrees, Air Methods no more than 5. Chapter says use the flattest ground.
- **Keep-out distance:** Beacon 200 ft from touchdown; Beacon ground-crew rule 100 ft; Air Methods 150 ft for crowds. Chapter says use the larger.
- **Two-handed seat arm position** is described differently (arm under back and thigh vs under shoulders and knees). Chapter uses the first and warns readers to practice.
- **Urgency levels:** the four-level grouping (stay, walk out, assisted carry-out, rapid evacuation) is our synthesis. NOLS uses a four-level scale of transport method; the Red Cross uses go fast / go slow. The chapter says this plainly.
- **Rope litter:** RopeWiki gave no written steps and Army/other sources did not cover it, so no rope-litter steps are given; only the American Alpine Club split-coil carry is mentioned (no steps; refers to instruction). "Backpack carry" and "clothes drag" by that exact name were not found; clothes drag is covered as the shoulder/clothing drag.
- **Spine handling:** Red Cross says do not move (support head, log roll if face down); WMS abstract says it avoids rigid collars/backboards, goal-based. Chapter gives the conservative advice and refers to Part 7 › Bones, Joints, Head & Spine.
- **Helicopter hand signals** are taken from the FAA circular (signalman-to-pilot signals); the chapter tells untrained people to stand clear.
- No doses or prescription drugs are given.

## Facts I could not verify, or where sources disagree
- **Oral fluids in shock (disagree).** Red Cross and MedlinePlus: nothing by mouth. American Alpine Club primer: oral fluids only if alert and able to swallow, max about 1 L/hour. ASRC protocol: give fluids to anyone more than a couple of hours from hospital unless choking/surgery/ileus risk. The chapter gives a cautious rule table and says the sources differ.
- **Open chest wound dressing (disagree).** TCCC (2013 onward) says vented seal first, non-vented second, monitor, burp if worse. WMS and Red Cross positions come only from the SIRIUSMEDx secondary summary; I did not open WMS or Red Cross text. The ASRC 2015 protocol still teaches three-sided tape and defibrillator pads as improvised seals. The chapter follows TCCC and says so.
- **Pushing back eviscerated bowel (disagree).** Red Cross: do not push in. TCCC article: trained responder may try briefly (60 seconds). ASRC: gently replace if evacuation over 1 hour. Chapter tells lay rescuers to follow the Red Cross.
- **Aspirin.** AHA: chew an aspirin while waiting. MedlinePlus: some experts suggest it, only after calling and if a doctor or 911 operator advises, never if allergic. The chapter reports both, adds "use as directed on the label", and gives no dose. I did not verify aspirin advice for stroke; the chapter says none of the opened stroke sources advise any drug, and gives "nothing by mouth" as the guide's own cautious rule.
- **Obstructive shock** is not listed as its own group in the lay wilderness sources I opened (AAC lists three). I added it from standard medical naming, tied to MedlinePlus's list of collapsed lung as a shock cause. Sepsis/septic shock definition: the CDC page I opened did not define septic shock; the chapter's one-line description is from general knowledge and is flagged only by wording ("infection that harms the whole body").
- **Unverified items kept as the guide's own cautious rules** (worded as such in the text): skip ibuprofen/aspirin if internal bleeding is possible; nothing by mouth after a stroke; the faint-versus-shock comparison table ("the guide's own summary"); "check back and sides for a second chest wound and seal it"; "dry the skin before sealing".
- **Flail chest field splinting:** no verified method found, so the chapter teaches none. **Needle decompression:** described only as a trained procedure; not taught.
- **Tension pneumothorax signs** come from TCCC text and the SIRIUSMEDx summary, not a lay first-aid agency.
- **Quadrant organs** (liver upper right, spleen upper left, appendix lower right) are supported by Merck (liver/spleen with lower rib fractures) and the NOLS appendicitis case; stomach and bowel labels are standard anatomy not tied to a source.
- Shoulder-pain-from-diaphragm explanation was dropped (unverified). Only the ASRC claim that new shoulder pain or sustained lightheadedness signals internal bleeding is kept, attributed to the protocol.
- Vital-sign thresholds (specific pulse or breathing numbers for shock) were deliberately omitted; only trends and the primer's 5 to 15 minute check interval are given. The trend-log figure uses made-up numbers and is labeled "EXAMPLE ONLY".
- No prescription-drug doses anywhere; ibuprofen, acetaminophen and aspirin are "use as directed on the label". Nitroglycerin appears only as "prescribed to them".

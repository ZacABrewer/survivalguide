## Could not verify / disagreements
- "3 minutes without air": no authoritative source gives an exact figure. MedlinePlus only says some brain cells start dying "within minutes." The chapter says so and does not assert 3 minutes as fact. Breath-hold figures were left out of the chapter.
- "3 hours exposure": no source supports it. The chapter calls it unsupported. The 30 to 60°F (-1 to 16°C) hypothermia figure used in the wet pyramid is carried from the existing shelter chapter (not re-verified here); it is flagged by cross-reference.
- Water-survival ranges (18-day record; 7-day desert case, 6 without water; "hours" at high heat; 1944 experiment origin) all come from Wikipedia, a secondary source, which cites Broyles et al. 1988 and a BBC Future piece I could not open. The chapter attributes them as such. The bar start of 2 hours for hot-desert water in Figure 1 is an illustrative "hours," not a sourced number.
- Starvation: ACUTE says no study shows how long starvation takes. The "up to about three months total fast / about one week dry fast" hunger-strike estimates are in ACUTE's page, drawn from one qualitative German study, so only used as a loose bar in Figure 1 (90 days), not in the text. Bar start of 1 week for food is illustrative.
- 382-day fast: ACUTE says medical monitoring and IV fluids; ScienceAlert says vitamin and electrolyte supplements plus calorie-free drinks and no IV mention. The chapter says only "medically supervised." The 1973 case report itself was not opened.
- Order of priorities: FM 21-76 (as returned by the fetch tool's extraction, not read line by line) lists food, water, shelter, fire, first aid, signals and says to adjust; the Multi-Service manual gives shelter, water, signals, fire (first 24 h) then tools and food; USCG gives recognition, inventory, shelter, signals, water, food, play. The chapter presents all three and says the sources disagree about food. The FM 21-76 ordering should be re-read in the PDF by a reviewer before publication, because it was relayed through a summarizing fetch.
- The "PLAN" and "PLW" acronyms and a "protection, location, water, food" SAR sequence mentioned in the brief could not be verified, so they are left out.
- Desert rationing: Army manual says ration water and conserve sweat; NPS says drink it, don't ration. Chapter shows both and says to ration effort, not drinking.
- Cold-water 1-10-1: Army page presents it as a rule; Cold Water Safety calls it oversimplified. The chapter shows both. The 25 to 30 times heat-loss figure is the Army page's wording (somewhat ambiguous about whether it refers to movement or immersion); the chapter attributes it to the Army.
- Sea pyramid: no Coast Guard/RNLI document gives a pyramid; the order is an adaptation of the USCG 7 steps plus the Army cold-water page and the Multi-Service manual. Signals are above water here (USCG), while the Multi-Service manual puts water above signals.
- Urban pyramid: built from the Red Cross earthquake page only (one source); "water heater tank" is carried from the Water chapter.
- Jungle list: from the Multi-Service manual (elevated shelter, purify water, daylight movement, avoid thickets/swamps). Ordering of items beyond that is this guide's synthesis.
- "Desert: do not eat if your water is short" is carried from the Water chapter's Phoenix Fire citation (not re-opened); the USCG card gives the same principle for rafts.
- Stay-or-go flow chart is a synthesis of the Multi-Service manual criteria and the NPS advice as summarized in the Signaling chapter (NPS page not re-opened).
- Bleeding "under 5 minutes": confirmed by two sources (US Army/MRDC article, American College of Surgeons).

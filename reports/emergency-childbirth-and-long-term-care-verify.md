# Verification report: Part 7A, Emergency Childbirth & Long-Term Care

Verifier: independent fact-check, November 16, 2026. WebSearch calls used: 5 of 10.

## Changes made
1. **Cord clamping timing.** Added ACOG (at least 30 to 60 seconds in a vigorous baby; Committee Opinion 684 abstract, opened via QxMD index) and Hesperian (no rush; tie once the pulse is gone) beside WHO's 1 to 3 minutes. The "at least 1 minute" advice is kept, as it satisfies all three.
2. **Cord ties.** WHO 2 cm / 5 cm confirmed on the WHO PCPNC chapter. Added Hesperian's "two fingers' width, then two more" rule of thumb. Added Hesperian's sterilizing advice (boil is best; otherwise flame or alcohol; a new razor blade is acceptable). The "longer is safer" tip and Ohio State's 6 to 8 in are kept.
3. **Cord prolapse.** The text said only knee-chest and no pushing. WHO PCPNC splits it by stage. Early labor: hips higher than shoulders and refer. Late labor: call for help, have her squat or sit upright, and push with contractions to deliver fast. Added the late-labor branch for when birth is minutes away.
4. **Postpartum bleeding.** Added WHO's position check (firm, midline, at or below the navel) and its forehead-versus-nose-tip firmness test, from the WHO Bleeding After Birth provider guide. Added Hesperian's advice to recheck and rub about every 15 minutes, and its rule that a big gush or ongoing flow is an emergency.
5. **Figure 5.** The womb was drawn well below the navel. Moved it so its top sits near the navel (WHO provider guide; Hesperian) and labeled that.
6. **Pressure-sore timing.** "Tissue can break down in as little as 2 hours (Merck)" is not on the Merck page, which says "more than a few hours." Changed to "within a few hours."
7. **Turning interval.** MedlinePlus (Preventing pressure ulcers) says every 1 to 2 hours, not "about 2 hours." The text now says at least every 2 hours, and hourly if skin stays red or the person is frail, and notes that sources differ. Added MedlinePlus advice: head of bed at or below 30 degrees, heels floated with a pillow under the calves (not the knees), and no donut cushions.
8. **Positioning in labor.** "StatPearls says never lay her flat" is now "not to keep her lying flat," with the reason (reduced blood flow to the womb), to match StatPearls.
9. **988.** Confirmed on the FCC 988 page: call or text 988, chat offered, 24/7, confidential. Veterans press 1 or text 838255. Spanish: press 2 or text AYUDA. ASL videophone. Routing by area code. Added Spanish, ASL, and routing. The FCC page also lists an LGBTQI+ "press 3" option; it was left out because that option was reported discontinued in 2025 and I could not confirm its current status. Removed "free" from the 988 line because FCC does not state it (the CDC page the writer cited said 24/7).
10. **Non-US crisis lines.** Replaced "could not open directories" with Find A Helpline. Its About page says it is free, run by ThroughLine, and verified with the helplines, covering more than 175 countries. The IASP crisis-centres page points to it.
11. **Figure 2, panel 1.** It was an unreadable abstract shape with a floating hand. Redrawn as a front view: the head at the opening, a hand resting on top, and a pad below, all labeled.
12. **Figure 3, panel 3.** The baby was hidden under an opaque blanket shape. Redrawn so the baby's head shows on the mother's chest, with the blanket over the body only, and labeled.
13. **Figure 7.** A center dot overlapped the "Turn every 2 hours" text. Removed it.
14. **Sources.** Added 7 opened URLs: ACOG CO 684 abstract (QxMD), two Hesperian pages, MedlinePlus 000147, FCC 988, Find A Helpline About, and IASP crisis centres.

## Claims verified (brief)
- **WHO PCPNC:** warm room at 25°C; loose or tight nuchal cord handling; shoulders rotate within 1 to 2 minutes, then down and up; ties at 2 and 5 cm, plus an extra tie if the cord oozes; nothing on the stump; cut the cord quickly if resuscitation is needed; check every 15 minutes in the first hour; never leave her alone; heavy bleeding means a pad soaked in under 5 minutes or a constant trickle; placenta still in at 1 hour means referral; breech: no pulling and let the baby hang; shoulder dystocia: knees to chest and suprapubic pressure; check for a second baby; no water or pre-lacteal feeds; severe depression or suicidal ideas mean seek care.
- **WHO PPH provider guide:** atony causes about 70% of cases; massage expels clots; visual estimates run low; a full bladder stops the womb contracting; shock signs.
- **CDC maternal warning signs:** one pad or more an hour; clots bigger than an egg; 100.4°F; the year after delivery; headache and vision signs; thoughts of harming self or baby.
- **CDC seizure first aid:** turn her on her side, put nothing in the mouth, time it; call 911 if she is pregnant.
- **Merck preeclampsia:** postpartum onset is most often within 4 days and can be up to 6 weeks; eclampsia is usually fatal if untreated.
- **MedlinePlus:** breech is about 3% of births.
- **StatPearls:** signs of imminent birth; contractions 2 minutes or less apart; newborn breathing 40 to 60 a minute; avoid vigorous throat suction; placenta in 5 to 15 minutes, up to 30; knee-chest for cord prolapse; turtle sign.
- **MSF:** about 10% of newborns need help; bulb suction no deeper than 2 cm and no longer than 5 seconds; bag-mask 30 to 60 breaths a minute; head neutral.
- **Ohio State and Lamaze** (secondary sources, labeled in the text): floss, boiled scissors, 6 to 8 in, no pushing too early, nursing releases oxytocin, never pull, no need to cut if help is 20 to 30 minutes away.
- **Merck pressure sores:** turn every 2 hours; reposition in a chair hourly; egg-crate foam does not help; keep the head of the bed low; stages; signs on dark skin; daily checks; keep off red areas.
- **CDC blood clots:** about half have no symptoms; PE signs mean get help immediately; get moving after bed rest.
- **WHO PFA:** 2011; WHO, War Trauma Foundation, World Vision; "humane, supportive and practical help."
- **Red Cross:** Disaster Distress Helpline 1-800-985-5990, call or text, free and 24/7; seek help after 2 weeks; children's reactions.
- **NPR:** direct question; plan means higher risk; urges ease in 24 to 48 hours; restrict means; HOME to 741741.
- **CDC:** out of sight or out of reach is "not secure storage."

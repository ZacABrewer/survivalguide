## Changes made (each with reason and source)

1. **Body signals (text, Fig 6, caption).** Removed "I found this only in Wikipedia." The section now attributes the Y/N signals to mountain rescue teams: Slovenian Mountain Rescue Association (GRZS) and Teton County SAR, as quoted by Buckrail. Y means "Yes, we need help." N, with one arm angled up and one angled down, means "No, we do not need help." The text labels this a rescue-team convention, not an FAA rule, and notes that US military manuals use a longer chart. I swapped the Wikipedia citation for the GRZS and Buckrail/TCSAR sources.
2. **Fig 6 redraw (coordinator request).** Each figure now has a shoulder bar, separate arms, and hands. The "No" figure clearly shows one arm raised and one lowered instead of a single slash.
3. **"Wave your arms even after you think you were seen."** Replaced with: if you need help, give a big, clear signal such as the Y, and do not wave at aircraft just to say hello. Both GRZS and TCSAR warn that a friendly wave can be mistaken for a call for help.
4. **Signal mirror method rewritten.** The old step "Face the sun, or turn up to 90 degrees away" got the geometry wrong (what matters is where the target is relative to the sun). The old step "flash up and down three times" had no source. The steps now follow the finger-V method from US Army Aviation Center Subcourse AV0664 (1993): mirror under the eye, target in the V, tilt until the spot is on your fingers and the light passes through, then rock the beam slowly. I added a short sighting-hole method from the same course (3 to 6 in from face) and the Army's distance of up to 160 km (100 mi). PopSci was replaced with AV0664.
5. **Fig 1 redraw.** The outstretched hand now points along the beam, the beam passes between the fingers, the mirror sits at the eye, and labels were moved into clear space.
6. **Arrow symbol.** The FAA AIM and ICAO Annex 12 say "Proceeding in this direction." The Army web text (aircav) and CPW say "Proceed in this direction." Both are now attributed, and the reader is told to follow the FAA meaning.
7. **Fig 3 caption.** Added the ICAO acknowledgment: wings rocked by day, landing lights flashed twice at night, and no reply means the pilot did not understand (ICAO Annex 12 appendix, as reproduced in Albania AIP GEN 3.6).
8. **Fig 2 (coordinator request).** Moved the distance label below its measurement line and enlarged the viewBox to 320.
9. **Fig 4 caption.** Removed "Leave a clear gap between the three letters." ITU-R M.1677 says nothing about SOS spacing, so the claim could not be verified. The caption now says to pause after the full pattern.
10. **Fig 5.** Moved the "406 MHz alert" label so the arrow no longer crosses it.
11. **Fig 7 (coordinator request).** Moved "Nose" into clear space above the fuselage and removed the stray line. Moved "Danger: tail rotor" outside the trapezoid with a leader line to the rotor.
12. **Accidental PLB activation.** Previously unsourced. It now gives NOAA's instructions: call the US Air Force rescue center at 1-800-851-3051 (PLB/ELT, 24 h) and have your Hex ID ready (NOAA SARSAT Prevent False Alerts).
13. **Landing zone size.** The text now attributes sizes to air-medical programs: Life Link III and Beacon say at least 100x100 ft, and DHART says 75x75 ft by day and 100x100 ft at night. It adds the military pickup area of about 150 ft across from FM 21-76-1 (1999). The NOLS wording was corrected: the handbook says about 60 ft, not "best minimum." The conservative advice is at least 100x100 ft, and more at night.
14. **Wind signal.** Now says stand just upwind of the zone, back to the wind, arms forward. Sources: Northland Rescue Helicopter captain in Wilderness magazine, and DHART.
15. **Wave-off.** Attribution corrected: cross arms over head repeatedly comes from DHART, an air-medical program, and sweeping Xs from a rescue-helicopter pilot.
16. **Marine VHF license.** "Check current rules" was replaced with the USCG NavCen rule: no FCC ship station license is needed for a vessel in US waters that uses only VHF, radar, or an EPIRB and is not required to carry a radio. A license is needed for international voyages or when a radio is required.
17. **Ham licensing.** Swapped Wikipedia for primary sources: 47 CFR 97.25 for the 10-year term, 47 CFR 97.501 and 97.503 for the required exams (Elements 2, 3, and 4 only, so no Morse test), and the FCC VEC page (exams given by volunteer examiners). ARRL VEC was kept.
18. **Trimmed redundancy.** Removed a duplicate "mirror on cockpit" do-not bullet, a duplicate "do not flash fast," and a duplicate GPS-details bullet that the "What to Say" section already covers.

## Claims verified
- **PLB:** 406 MHz only; manual activation; at least 24 h battery; GPS to about 100 m; 121.5 MHz not detected since 2009; registration free and required by 47 CFR 80/87/95; 888-212-7283; renew every 2 years; 24-h emergency contact; RLS light means only that the alert was received and forwarded. Sources: NOAA emerbcns and Registration Brochure.
- **FRS:** licensed by rule, 22 channels, 2 W on ch 1–7 and 15–22, 0.5 W on 8–14, all shared with GMRS, over-limit radios are GMRS devices. **GMRS:** license required, 10 yr, age 18+, family may operate, range 1–25 mi. **CB:** no license; ch 9 = 27.065 MHz for emergencies and traveler assistance. Source: FCC.
- **NOAA Weather Radio:** 7 frequencies, 162.400–162.550 MHz (NWS).
- **Marine:** Ch 16 is the distress, safety, and calling channel. MAYDAY order checked. Hoaxes, including MAYDAY radio checks, can be prosecuted (USCG NavCen).
- **Morse:** all 26 letters and 10 digits in the table match ITU-R M.1677 exactly. Dash = 3 dots. Fig 4 shows S as 3 short and O as 3 long, which is correct.
- **Ground-to-air:** symbols and meanings match the FAA AIM, ICAO Annex 12, and Army text. FAA says at least 10 ft high. Army says 1 m x 6 m (1:6). "If in doubt, use SOS," and dug or stamped snow shows dark from the air (FAA AIM).
- **Army FM 21-76 text:** mirror cautions, flash toward engine sound, three fires about 25 m apart, one fire acceptable when alone, white and black smoke materials, three smoke columns.
- **Other agencies:** CPW (glass mirror visible 20–25 mi, fires 30–50 ft apart, apex fire kept burning). NPS (trip plan, stay put, no fire in high fire danger, whistle 3x, SOS flashlight drains battery, flag someone down to call 911). USFS (itinerary contents, stay put, send at least two people, three blasts/shouts/smokes/flashes, phones likely won't work). AHS (water exception). SDMRT (four Hug-A-Tree rules, "nobody will be angry"). Apple (iPhone 14+, sky/horizon view, try a call first, demo, Medical ID). FCC (call if you can, bounce-back). FEMA (texts get through, low power or airplane mode). REI.
- **Landing zone:** slope under 10° and people kept 200 ft back (Beacon). Approach only from front or sides in pilot view, never from the rear; avoid shining lights at the aircraft (Life Link III). No flares (DHART). Not from uphill, tail rotor hard to see, anchored bright markers, avoid holes under ridges (NOLS).

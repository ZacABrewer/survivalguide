## Could NOT verify, left out, or weak
- Köppen codes, forest-type names and "over 6,000 ft" elevation: stated from general knowledge, not from an opened source.
- Friction-fire species (basswood, tulip poplar, cedar, willow, cottonwood): no authoritative species list found. FM 21-76 gives only hardwood drill / softwood board, so the chapter says to test local woods. Fatwood omitted. Birch bark as tinder: NC State only confirms it peels in papery layers. Eastern redcedar bark as tinder is my suggestion ("worth testing"), not a sourced use.
- Maple sap season (late winter to early spring): Cornell and Ontario pages failed to load, so no source. NC State gives only the 32:1 ratio.
- Omitted for lack of a verified source: violets, chickweed, wood sorrel, blackberry/raspberry, goldenrod, black widow, wild parsnip details (cross-referenced to the carrot-family chapter), pokeweed cooking method, firewood-movement rules, Ontario and Quebec ramp rules, Ontario Crown land rules.
- Tick markings (lone star white spot, dog tick pattern): not found in an opened source. Figure 7 shows range, diseases and removal only. CDC pages opened do not state how many hours of attachment are needed for Lyme.
- Acorn leaching steps and times: only a popular source (Farmers' Almanac). Flagged in the caption. The tannin point (white oaks milder) is from the same page.
- "Boiling or filtering does not fix algal-bloom water" and "boiling does not remove metals or acid": the CDC and USGS pages opened are silent. Stated conservatively and as "CDC does not say".
- Copperhead range and habitat: the MDC copperhead page would not load, so the card covers ID and behavior only (Clemson, NJ DEP). Cottonmouth range is Missouri-only; other states' ranges not checked.
- Ramps in Great Smoky Mountains NP: status comes from a 2010 article and a 2015 article about a proposed rule; current status unknown. Penn State's page says nothing about park limits.
- "Narrow valleys can fill fast" flash-flood remark is general inference; the NWS page gave no timing.
- Zones and frost dates: no numeric ranges given; USDA map definition only.
- Jack-o'-lantern habit (clusters on wood) comes from Wikipedia, a secondary source; the MDC chanterelle page (no true gills, not on decaying wood) and NAMA (symptoms, chanterelle mix-up) back the main points.

## Where sources disagree
- Lily of the valley: NC State rates poison severity low; Penn State calls it deadly poisonous. Chapter treats it as dangerous.
- Several NC State pages tag contact dermatitis while their data field says "no" (lily of the valley, false hellebore, bittersweet nightshade); chapter advises gloves for pokeweed, yarrow and pawpaw only where the page says so.
- Serviceberry and mulberry fruit seasons are inconsistent within the NC State pages; the calendar uses broad months.

## Where sources disagree (chapter gives the conservative choice)
- Walking ice minimum: Minnesota DNR and Lifesaving Society say 4 in (10 cm); Canadian Red Cross says 15 cm (6 in), 20 cm for a group. Snowmobile: DNR 5-7 in, LSS 5 in, Red Cross 25 cm (10 in). The chapter shows all three and advises the thicker number.
- Who stands upstream in a group river crossing: Mountaineers says strongest; Clever Hiker says heaviest; Army manual says heaviest downstream and lightest upstream. The chapter says so and suggests strongest in front.
- Hip belt: most sources say unbuckle in fast water; one SectionHiker commenter prefers it buckled with a heavy load; the Mountaineers linked-arm method keeps it buckled. The chapter gives a conditional rule.
- Falling through ice: lifesaving.com says to turn back the way you came; Red Cross says do not climb out at the break spot. The chapter reconciles them.
- Army manual: "do not ration water" in the desert chapter and "ration water until a source is found" in the medicine chapter. The chapter uses the desert chapter.

## Facts I could not verify well
- "Never tie a rope to a person in current" appears only in SectionHiker reader comments among opened sources, and the chapter says so. I did not open American Whitewater's rope guidance.
- Horseshoe pack and travois: no authoritative or secondary source opened. The chapter describes both in general terms only, with no measurements, in a labeled "Source note" tip. Consider cutting or sourcing them.
- Pack fitting details (torso 15-22 in, sternum strap 1 in below collarbones, load lifters about 45 degrees) come from RockyMap only. The 20% and 10% pack weight guide is from two secondary sources (REI, Great Walks).
- No source gave a slush or rotten-ice thickness rule. The chapter says so and advises staying off.
- Leech removal: not covered (no source opened). Only prevention is covered, from the Army manual.
- The Army manual's "early morning travel in avalanche terrain" is quoted as a rule of thumb only, with a caution.
- Army water figures (19 L/day at 43°C; 0.5 L/h below 38°C and 1 L/h above) are from the manual and are attributed to it. They are for hard work.
- Snowshoe and ski technique beyond the Army's brief points was not found in sources. The chapter says to learn it in person.

## Facts not verified, or weak spots
- Oxygen safety: no oxygen-safety page could be opened. The chapter says only that oxygen makes fires burn faster (general knowledge, not sourced), and tells readers to get exact safe distances and cylinder run times from their supplier. No numbers given. Flagged in the text.
- Older-adult heat/cold vulnerability: no page opened. The text makes no numeric claim; it says to check often, to ask a pharmacist about medicine effects, and refers to Part 7 › Environmental Emergencies.
- Infant cold protection: no source opened. Only the CDC/AAP overheating cue (sweating, hot chest) is stated.
- Pregnancy: kept to planning only (records, routes, call provider); no clinical claims.
- Utility "medical baseline" lists: not verified. Written as "ask your electric utility whether it keeps a list". Registries: FEMA (Florida example) and ASTHO (false-sense-of-security warning).
- PETS Act: only the APHIS background was verified (passed after Katrina; FEMA does the work). Its specific provisions were not opened and are not described.
- Emotional support animals: CDC says the Fair Housing Amendments Act requires disaster shelters to allow them; the ADA National Network page does not address them. Single-source, flagged in a legal callout. ADA National Network states the rule for government-run (Title II) shelters; CDC says "shelters must allow service animals" more broadly.
- Wandering: Alzheimer's Association says call 911 after 15 minutes and about 60% wander; the chapter advises calling sooner in bad weather or near water/roads (author's conservative advice).
- Insulin: FDA gives 59-86 F (15-30 C) up to 28 days for vials or cartridges. CDC gives no day count and only says above 86 F is less effective. Chapter uses the FDA figure and tells readers to check their own product label. The tip "do not set insulin against ice" is an inference from "do not use frozen insulin". FDA switching doses and pump limits were deliberately left out (prescription dosing rule).
- Fridge/freezer power-outage times (4 h, 40 F) are from CDC infant feeding page.
- Some chapter tips are unsourced common-sense advice (spare glasses, daily check-in signal, stair/building manager question, backup person for pets).
- The caring-for chapter cross-references the Kits & Gear generator advice (CDC/CPSC in that chapter's report) rather than citing it again.

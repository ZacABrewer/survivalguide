## Disagreements / unverified
- Boiling altitude cutoff: CDC 6,500 ft; EPA 5,000 ft (its "1,000 m" is inconsistent with 5,000 ft); Colorado State 2,000 m; Army FM 21-76 adds 1 min per 300 m or 10 min anywhere. Chapter uses 3 min above 5,000 ft and "if unsure, 3 min".
- Chlorine dioxide vs Cryptosporidium: only Colorado State says 4 hours; CDC says "several hours" and to follow the label. CDC gives no iodine dose; iodine dose is from EPA and FM 21-76.
- Filter pore size for protozoa: CDC says 1 micron / NSF 53 or 58; Colorado State says under 0.1 micron.
- Ready.gov rejects iodine; EPA lists it as backup. Ready.gov bleach 5.25-6% only; EPA gives 6% and 8.25% table (used).
- Storage duration: Ready.gov says "several days"; archived 2012 CDC page says 3 days, ideally 2 weeks. The FEMA "2 weeks" figure was not confirmed on Ready.gov. Six-month rotation confirmed by both Ready.gov and CDC (archived).
- Drinking to thirst (WMS, NPS, Phoenix) vs. Army "thirst is not reliable"; both presented.
- SODIS: CDC Yellow Book says 6 h / 2 days; Akvopedia (Eawag-based) adds 30 NTU, 1-2 L PET, shaking; the PMC study says SODIS does not reliably inactivate viruses or protozoa. CDC's own backcountry page gives no SODIS procedure. The Eawag manual and CDC SODIS page could not be fetched.
- Yields: Army solar still 0.5-1 L/24 h; transpiration bag 0.3-1 L/day, solar still 0.3-0.6 L/day, dew 0.1-0.3 L/day and rain 5-10 L/event come from a medRxiv preprint (not peer reviewed, one reviewer, blank table).
- Ice vs snow "twice as much for half the heat" is a single guide's claim via Caltech; hedged in text. No authoritative snow:water ratio found, so none given.
- Outer-bend digging depth (3-6 ft) is from Phoenix Fire only. Cliff seeps and potholes are from Backpacker (magazine, not a primary authority).
- Not verified, so omitted: urine "saltier than seawater" claim, cactus legal protection, NASEM daily water intake figures, exact Army desert-finding text (ATP 3-50.21 fetch returned no usable text).
- Backpacker says to drink questionable water if no alternative; the chapter takes the more conservative line (treat first).
- Cross-reference "Part 7 > First Aid" is based on plan.md part numbering.

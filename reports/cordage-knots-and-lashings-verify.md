# Verification report: cordage-knots-and-lashings
Verifier: independent fact-check, 2026-11-11. File: /home/claude/survival-site/content/cordage-knots-and-lashings.html
Searches used: 2 of 8 (the rest was WebFetch).

## Changes made (reason, source)
1. **Knot strength.** Dropped the Wikipedia "40% to 80%" figure. The section now gives only the CMC Rescue test ranges: 49–82% overall, double fisherman's about 80%, figure-8 loop 54–80%, bowline 58–75%. They are labeled as rough figures for new, clean rope in a lab, and the text says worn, wet, or badly tied rope can do worse. The CMC paper does not say whether the rope was new, so the wording is deliberately the best case. Bowline "two-thirds" changed to the CMC 58–75%. (CMC Rescue, "Rescue Knot Efficiency Revisited", NSS Vertical Section.)
2. **Tail length.** Removed the unsourced "4 to 8 inches (10–20 cm)". Now says to leave a long tail, and a much longer one for critical loads (Animated Knots bowline, sheet bend and double fisherman's pages).
3. **Square knot check.** Replaced "wrap leans the same way" with: interlocked bights lie flat, each end sits beside its own standing part, and both ends are on the same side. Ends sticking out crosswise mean a granny. The deaths line is now attributed to Ashley as cited by Animated Knots ("probably cost more lives…"), not "deaths have been blamed". (Animated Knots reef knot; Wikipedia reef knot for the same-side rule.)
4. **Constrictor steps were wrong** ("pass under the two wraps"). Rewritten: one turn crossing the standing part (the riding turn), around again, then over the standing part and tucked under the riding turn so both ends come out between the turns. NetKnots (a retailer) was replaced by ScoutWiki.
5. **Trucker's hitch.** The text now says to pull the end back toward the second anchor. The "1.6:1" figure is attributed to Animated Knots as "may be". **Figure 9 was wrong**: the free end was pulled away from the hook, which gives no mechanical advantage, and "standing part" labeled the wrong segment. Fixed the return path, the arrow, the label and the caption.
6. **Timber hitch steps.** Removed "and back through the loop". The end now wraps around its own part at least 3 times. Replaced the Wikipedia "5+ turns" with "use extra turns on slick synthetic rope" (Animated Knots).
7. **Round turn and two half hitches.** Removed "can be tied and untied under load"; Animated Knots does not say that. Now says the round turn takes the strain while you tie, and to tie both half hitches the same way.
8. **Prusik.** Added Petzl's rule: match cord size to the rope's diameter and keep a clear difference. Kept 5–6 mm cord (Animated Knots) and test near the ground. Removed the unsourced "icy rope" claim.
9. **Clove hitch.** Removed the Wikipedia-only "poor on square posts".
10. **Figure-8 loop.** "Jams after heavy loads" softened to "can be hard to untie after a heavy load".
11. **Manila.** Changed "weak when wet" to "do poorly in salt water, shrink when wet, rot if stored damp" (Navy Marlinespike PDF).
12. **Plants.**
    - Milkweed: added a skin-hazard badge and the note that its sap can cause rash and eye irritation (NC State Extension).
    - Yucca: added that leaf tips have caused eye injuries and a few people react on the skin (Botanical Dermatology Database).
    - Basswood: inner bark use and edible young leaves now attributed to USDA.
    - The warning callout was updated to match.
13. **Lashings.**
    - Tripod rope: changed to 12½ ft (3.8 m) of 1/4-inch rope for Scout staves (scoutpioneering).
    - Floor lashing: step 2 clarified (over the spar inside the support, under the support, back over outside).
14. **Safety list.** "Do not test in the direction a fall would load it" was confusing. Now: test with a firm, controlled pull, and never test a knot by falling on it or hanging your weight on it off the ground (Animated Knots). Added "follow the maker's limits" to rope retirement.
15. **Small fixes.** Lede now says "18 working knots" instead of "about two dozen" (18 are covered). Fixed an empty `class=""`. URL-encoded the spaces in the CMC PDF link. Verified date set to November 11, 2026.
16. **Sources.**
    - Removed: Wikipedia knot strength, bowline, clove, taut-line, trucker's and timber pages, and NetKnots.
    - Added: ScoutWiki constrictor and taut-line; Animated Knots midshipman's and round turn; Petzl prusik; NC State milkweed; scoutpioneering square lashing.
    - Wikipedia figure-eight loop and bast fibre remain (non-safety claims only).

## Claims verified (brief)
- **Knots against Animated Knots:** bowline steps, weaknesses and climbing backup; sheet bend (steps, tails on the same side, double sheet bend, slack-rope warning); figure-8 stopper; figure-8 follow-through and parallel-strand check; alpine butterfly (hand-wrap steps, uses, isolating damage); clove hitch (slips, not for use alone); rolling hitch (steps, pull in line, fails on slick line); double fisherman's (2 wraps, jams, inspect); highwayman's (steps; dangerous for rappelling); timber hitch (3 turns, falls apart without tension, killick); reef (crossings, never for critical loads); safety page (50% rule, 5:1–12:1 design factor, 12:1 for live loads, handling rules); trucker's (3:1 theory, about 1.6:1 real).
- **Other knot sources:** taut-line steps (ScoutWiki BSA version, plus Animated Knots midshipman's note on slick line); cleat hitch steps and "extra turns slow casting off", overhand used sparingly, fiber properties including nylon stretching up to 40% (Navy Marlinespike PDF).
- **Lashings against scoutpioneering:** square (clove hitch, 3 wraps, 2 fraps, clove hitch; rope = 3 ft per inch of combined spar diameter; 1/4" vs 3/8" rope), diagonal (timber hitch, 3+3 wraps, 2–3 fraps, clove hitch), shear (clove hitch about 6 in from the end, 8–10 wraps, 2 fraps, clove hitch on the other spar, loose-wrap options, A-frame), tripod (6–8 wraps, two sets of fraps run opposite ways, cross outside legs under the middle), round (about 10 in overlap, clove hitch, 8–10 wraps, no fraps, two lashings), floor (steps, 50 ft for a 6-ft platform).
- **Plants:**
  - Dogbane: NC State rates it highly toxic in all parts (cardiac glycosides); sap blisters skin and causes dermatitis. USDA covers the fall harvest and about 5 stalks per foot of cord.
  - Milkweed: glycosides in all parts; fiber processing (USDA).
  - Nettle: stinging hairs (K-State). Season, steps, shrinkage and the pollutant warning (WA OSPI).
  - Basswood: inner bark (USDA); wet twine knots tightening as they dry (USFS).
  - Banana yucca processing and western red cedar bark (USFS).
  - Agave: sisal and henequen; sap causes severe dermatitis (Desert Museum, Botanical Dermatology Database).
  - Hemp and flax: wording kept generic ("check local rules").
- **Rope inspection and retirement:** inspection list and loop test, middle-of-rope damage, acid damage that does not show, falls (Mammut). Retirement intervals and marking or cutting up retired rope (REI).
- **Life-safety message:** present at the top and in the safety section.

## Still uncertain or notes
- **Figure 10 (timber hitch)** is still weak. The "noose" does not visibly circle the log. The text steps are correct, but the figure should be redrawn in a later pass.
- **Figure 11 (prusik):** the cord-loop legs leave from the bottom of the coil. On a real prusik they come out of the middle. It is minor and labeled "simplified", but worth a redraw.
- **Figure 4:** a schematic of interlocked bights. I could not view a reference image because the browser needed approval and no one answered. A human visual check against Animated Knots is still recommended for Figures 4 and 5.
- **Inline `style="stroke-width:…"`** is used on about 110 shapes, the original ones and my new ones alike. The CSS classes override presentation attributes, so this is the only way to draw thick rope with spec classes. There are no colors in it. Flag it if the build bans style attributes.
- **Length:** about 5,100 words including sources and captions, which is above the spec range. I left it, since that is a structural call for the editor.
- **Bowline under cyclic loading:** sources differ. Animated Knots says it tolerates cyclic loads "fairly well"; other references say it can work loose. I kept the more cautious "can work loose when slack or when the load comes and goes".

## Changes made (reason, source)

1. **UET steps corrected against the manuals.** I read FM 21-76-1 (irp.fas.org) and FM 3-05.70, Fig. 9-5 (irp.fas.org).
   - Contact test: the chapter said to "rub the plant's juice" on the skin. The manuals say to place a piece of the plant there so the juice touches the skin. Fixed.
   - Final portion: the chapter said only "a larger portion". FM 3-05.70 says 0.25 cup; the FM 21-76-1 scan drops the denominator. Changed to "about 1/4 cup (60 mL)".
   - Added "leaf" to the list of plant parts to test (both manuals include leaves).
   - Smell step now reads "strong or acid odor", matching the manual.
   - Split the fast and the skin test into separate steps (spec: one action per step). Added the manual's "no burning, itching, numbness, or stinging" condition before swallowing.
   - Said that the test appears in FM 3-05.70 and FM 21-76-1. ATP 3-50.21 was not opened.
2. **Induce-vomiting instruction.** The chapter now says the Army manuals tell you to induce vomiting, that Poison Control says not to, and to call Poison Help at 1-800-222-1222 or 911. Sources: poison.org "Need Immediate Assistance" (do not induce vomiting; call 911 for collapse, seizure, trouble breathing, or can't be woken) and HRSA Poison Help (number, free, 24/7).
   - Applied the same wording to the top Warning callout.
   - Added a "Do not make anyone vomit" item to the UET Do-not box.
   - Renamed "Poison Control" to "Poison Help" where it means the phone line.
3. **"Last resort" attribution.** The H3 said "Why experts treat it as a last resort". Kirtley never uses that phrase, and neither manual says it. Retitled the H3 "Why this guide treats it as a last resort" and added a line saying the label is the guide's own advice. Kirtley's points are now attributed accurately; most of them come from his replies to readers on the article page.
4. **UET limits made concrete.**
   - Death cap (*Amanita phalloides*, amatoxins): poison.org's case had vomiting at about 12 hours, which is later than the test's 8-hour wait.
   - Water hemlock: a 2–3 cm piece of root can kill (CDC MMWR), so the test dose itself can be fatal.
5. **Army red-flag list.** Checked all 11 items and the exceptions against FM 21-76-1; all are present and correct.
   - Rewrote the exceptions to match the manual: dandelion; prickly pear and thistles; aggregate berries (blackberries, dewberries) "edible", test all other berries.
   - Added that the manual admits the list may rule out some edible plants.
   - Added one sentence on the different FM 3-05.70 list: discolored sap, bitter or soapy taste, carrot-like foliage, three-leaved growth.
   - Corrected the description "the Army's survival manual". FM 21-76-1 is the multi-service SERE guide.
   - Fixed the misquote "seeds in pods" to "seeds inside pods".
   - Table exceptions are correct. Shortened the table cells.
6. **Mustard "often cyanogenic" claim removed** from the mustard paragraph and the Myth callout. It came from a broad family description (UAF notes) and is misleading as foraging safety advice; the family's typical chemistry is glucosinolates. Replaced it with a warning not to trust family-wide edibility claims. Elpel's pages claim every mustard is edible and any mint is safe to sample, so the chapter now says not to rely on such claims.
7. **Mustard stamens.** Confirmed six stamens, four tall and two short, with UAF notes and Elpel's Brassicaceae page (added as a source). UAF says not every species shows this; the chapter now says so.
8. **Mint family.** Confirmed square stems, opposite and often decussate leaves, two-lipped flowers, and often aromatic, with Open Textbook BC and Elpel. Confirmed the non-mint square-stem look-alikes (Elpel). Wording tightened.
9. **Death camas.**
   - Rephrased the Montana State sentence, which closely copied the source.
   - Added USDA ARS Poisonous Plant Lab: the bulbs have caused severe illness and death in humans.
   - Expanded the warning callout: smell on your hands or from nearby crushed plants, crush plants one at a time, clean hands between plants. These caveats are this guide's own conservative advice. No extension source I could open states them; see "Still uncertain".
10. **Fasciola.** Reworded to match CDC: raw watercress and other freshwater plants carrying larvae; water, or vegetables washed or irrigated with it; avoid eating them raw, especially from grazing areas. Added "Do not eat wild water plants raw."
11. **USDA link.** Replaced the staging host with the production URL https://www.usda.gov/sites/default/files/documents/foraging-harvesting-indigenous-wild-plants-best-practices.pdf (opened and confirmed). Corrected the title to "Best Practices for Foraging and Harvesting Indigenous and Wild Plants".
12. **Lectins.**
   - Replaced the weakly hosted lectin review (fundacionbengoa.org scan) with the FDA Bad Bug Book, Phytohaemagglutinin chapter. FDA says 4–5 raw kidney beans can cause illness within 1–3 hours. It advises soaking at least 5 hours and boiling in fresh water at least 30 minutes, and says slow cookers may not get hot enough.
   - This is more conservative than the review's "100°C for 20 min".
13. **Plant-ID apps.** Checked the claims against Scimex (six apps, 38 species, best two wrong about 1 in 8, worst results from leaves alone, caution for toxic species) and Utah Poison Control (3 apps, 5.8–59% for poisonous plants). Only the attribution wording changed: "Irish and UK researchers (including the University of Galway)".
14. **Smaller fixes.**
   - Removed "runoff", which MSU does not mention, and merged the unsourced "old industrial sites" bullet into the MSU-sourced contamination bullet.
   - Silique example: "radish" became "cabbage". Radish fruit does not split into two valves.
   - Ergot: "most common host" became "especially affects rye", matching Britannica.
   - Carrot-family cross-reference now uses the required "(see Part 4 › …)" format. Added a cross-reference to Part 9 › Signaling, Rescue & Communication.
   - "Ask three questions" became "Ask these questions" (there are four bullets).
   - The poison.org publisher is now listed as National Capital Poison Center.
   - Dichotomous-key text no longer attributes a claim that Open Textbook BC does not make.
   - Trimmed the legal note and other passages to offset the safety additions.
15. **SVG fixes.**
   - Fig. 7 (mustard flower): petals and sepals were drawn so that the stamens lined up with the petals. In the mustard floral plan, the sepals alternate with the petals, the long-stamen pairs sit opposite the median sepals, and the short stamens sit opposite the lateral sepals. I rotated the petals to the diagonals and the sepals to the cross positions, so the existing stamen dots are now correct. I re-laid the leaders so they don't cross, and the sepal label now reads "between petals". Re-rendered and checked.
   - Fig. 8 (key): the "maybe a mint" end box used the green "safe" stroke (ss), which implies the plant is safe. Changed it to the accent stroke (sa). Couplet 1b now reads "Alternate, whorled, or basal"; whorled leaves had fallen through both choices. The caption now says the key never proves a plant is edible.
   - Fig. 2: added the twig line and bud dot to the palmate and bipinnate panels, to match the caption.
   - Fig. 4: the filaments now arise from the receptacle instead of mid-ovary, and the filament leader end was moved onto the filament.
   - Figs. 1, 3, 5, 6 checked by reading the coordinates and rendering in headless Chrome. Fig. 3 margins match the UT Austin definitions. Fig. 5 inflorescences match Wayne's Word. Fig. 6's decussate pairs match Open Textbook BC. No changes needed.

## Claims verified (brief)
- FM 21-76-1 avoid list (11 items) and its exceptions.
- FM 3-05.70 avoid list and UET steps; more than 24 hours per plant part; skip plants that are not abundant.
- poison.org: mushroom symptoms delayed many hours; the 12-hour case; liver and kidney failure; experts only for ID.
- CDC MMWR 1992 Maine: 2–3 cm of root can be fatal; three bites, death about 3 hours later; most toxic native plant; no antidote; most misidentification deaths.
- CDC Fasciola transmission and prevention.
- MSU Extension: 3 guides; one-third harvest limit; pokeweed in 3 changes of water, roots and berries highly toxic; wrist touch test; public lands; kudzu and dandelion; contamination sites.
- USDA Black Elk/Iron Cloud guide content.
- 36 CFR 2.1: plants protected; superintendent may designate fruits, berries, and nuts for personal use with limits; no sale; mushrooms not mentioned.
- Montana death camas: odor difference; all parts toxic; no known treatment.
- Utah Poison Control: death camas look-alikes; mentor advice; the app study; 1-800-222-1222.
- OSU ANR-102: wild carrot "generally safe" and can irritate skin; poison hemlock hollow, hairless, purple-spotted stem with musty odor; wild carrot solid, hairy stem with carrot odor.
- SDSU nightshade: solanine; symptoms; ripe berries only in limited amounts.
- Britannica ergot.
- UT Austin leaf terms, including lobes more than about 1/4 of the way to the midvein.
- Wayne's Word inflorescence definitions.
- Open Textbook BC key-use advice and family traits.
- HRSA Poison Help: 1-800-222-1222, free, 24/7.

## Still uncertain
- **Death camas smell caveats.** The hands and nearby-plants caveat has no extension source; no opened extension page states it. It is kept as unattributed conservative advice. A follow-up search is recommended to add a citation.
- **Ergot source.** Still Britannica (a reputable encyclopedia, not a primary authority). I could not open an extension or NIH page on ergotism. The advice that depends on it (never eat spurred grain) is conservative.
- **ATP 3-50.21** (the current Army survival manual) was not opened. The UET text comes from FM 3-05.70 and FM 21-76-1.
- **App study.** Only the Scimex news summary was opened, not the PLOS ONE paper itself.
- **Unsourced common botany.** The fruit-type table, the leaflet-versus-leaf bud test, the rose, aster, and grass descriptions, and "hemlocks lack spines or milky sap" are all standard botany with no source. None of them is safety-critical in isolation.

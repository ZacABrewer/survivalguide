Verifier pass, 16 Nov 2026. WebSearch used: 10 of 10. The body is now about 5,500 words, over the spec's 3,500 limit, because the user asked for the gaps to be filled. 8 figures. Sources: 20 were kept and 26 added.

## Changes made
1. **Homemade ORS (priority 1).** The recipe is now 1 liter of clean water + 6 level teaspoons of sugar + ½ level teaspoon of salt. It is confirmed by WHO EMRO's leaflet for the public (WHO-EM/CSR/594/E), which is primary, and matches the Rehydration Project "made at home" page. Removed the unsourced gram estimates (about 25 g, about 2 g), because ½ tsp of table salt is closer to 3 g. Added the older WHO 8 + 1 formula as historical, citing the PubMed abstract (1988). Added a measuring-spoon tip, because that review found pinch and tablespoon errors made home mixes dangerous. Updated Figure 2 to match and fixed the stirring stick, which floated above the glass.
2. **ORS use.** Added WHO IMCI Plan A amounts after each loose stool (50–100 mL under age 2, 100–200 mL age 2 and up) and the 10-minute wait after vomiting. Added WHO/UNICEF's rule not to keep mixed solution more than 24 h. Added WHO's zinc recommendation with no dose ("from a health worker").
3. **Dehydration signs.** Added WHO's severe and some-dehydration signs: alertness, sunken eyes, drinking, skin pinch (2 seconds or more). Severe dehydration needs IV fluids (WHO fact sheet).
4. **Cholera-type stools (new subsection).** Covers rice-water, milky-white stools (CDC), 1 in 10 severe, and death possible within hours (CDC) or within 24 h (WHO EMRO). Start ORS at once and keep giving it on the way to care. No sugary drinks (CDC).
5. **Fever without a thermometer (new subsection).** Two systematic reviews: Indian Pediatrics 2017 found sensitivity 87.5% and specificity 54.6%. J Trop Pediatr 2008 found 89.2% and 50%. So "feels normal" fairly rules fever out, and "feels hot" is unreliable. The comparison method is labeled as this guide's suggestion.
6. **WHO fast-breathing limits (priority 5).** Text and Figure 3 now show WHO IMCI: under 2 months 60+ (count again), 2–11 months 50+, 12 months–5 years 40+. Removed the "underweight child: limits 5 lower" line, which I could not verify because the MSF page was blocked for me. Added the IMCI general danger signs. The caption notes MSF's 1-month cut-off.
7. **Adult breathing rate (new).** Normal 12–20 a minute (StatPearls "Tachypnea"; MedlinePlus gives 12–18). Over 20 is tachypnea. Over 25 independently predicts higher mortality in hospital (StatPearls "Abnormal Respirations"). The chapter uses over 25 at rest as an evacuation trigger.
8. **Steam (new).** Cochrane review: 6 trials, mixed results, not recommended as routine treatment. Added a scald warning, especially for children. The scald risk is stated as common sense, not cited.
9. **Eyes (priority 3).**
   - Flushing time is now "at least 15 minutes" (Prevent Blindness, MedlinePlus) or 15–20 (Better Health). The text says aim for 20.
   - Added: injured eye down and to the side, shower if both eyes are splashed, and remove a contact after flushing if it did not wash out (MedlinePlus).
   - New step list for flipping the eyelid over a cotton swab (MedlinePlus), with do-nots: no swab on the colored part, no tools.
   - For an embedded object, loosely cover both eyes (MedlinePlus). For blood pooling, cover both eyes.
   - New "when to patch" table: do not patch an abrasion (AAFP); shield a penetrating injury.
   - Removed the unsourced "do not bandage after a chemical burn" from Figure 4 and replaced it with "get urgent care, bring label".
   - Figure 5 text now matches the MedlinePlus method.
   - Figure 6: fixed the "Tape to cheekbone" label that overlapped the face and added "loosely cover the good eye too".
10. **Dental.**
    - Knocked-out tooth (priority 4): storage is now milk first (Mayo, Cleveland Clinic, ODA), then saliva (Mayo), then salt water or a tooth-saving kit (Cleveland Clinic). Added ODA timing: within 10 min fair chance, after 1 h poor. Moved the tooth into the milk in Figure 7.
    - New subsections:
      - Lost filling: sugarless gum or OTC dental cement.
      - Lost crown: cement, toothpaste or denture adhesive, no super glue.
      - Broken tooth.
      - Toothache: no aspirin on the gum, no heat.
      - Clove oil: insufficient evidence; FDA downgraded eugenol; repeated use can damage gums and pulp; unsafe by mouth for children. Source is RxList, based on Natural Medicines. **Secondary source, labeled as such.**
      - Tooth abscess: signs and warm salt-water rinse (½ tsp in 8 oz).
      - Ludwig angina danger-sign callout: under-tongue or neck swelling, trouble swallowing, drooling, trouble breathing, which is an emergency (MedlinePlus).
11. **Skin.**
    - New fungal subsection: ringworm (CDC) and athlete's foot (MedlinePlus). OTC antifungal as directed, continued 1–2 weeks after clearing. No steroid cream on possible ringworm. Get care if not better in 2–4 weeks or if bacterial signs appear.
    - Cellulitis and abscess are now sourced to MedlinePlus: pen line, elevation, red streaks, fever, near-eye infection needs hospital, warm moist compresses, do not squeeze. The cross-reference to Part 7 › Bleeding, Wounds, Burns & Infection is kept.

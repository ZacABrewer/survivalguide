# Verification report: priorities (Part 1 Ch 2)

## Changes made
1. FM 21-76 priority order corrected (table row + paragraph). Re-read the SJSU PDF: Ch 1 says a survival pattern "must include food, water, shelter, fire, first aid, and signals placed in order of importance", then gives a cold example that starts with fire and shelter, and says to change the pattern as the environment changes. That list is not a ranking. The chapter had presented it as ranked with food first. Now reads "Not ranked..." and the food-disagreement paragraph is rewritten.
2. Desert rationing corrected. FM 21-76 Ch 13 says "Do not ration your water!" and warns that rationing causes heat casualties; it says to conserve sweat and not to eat when water is short. NPS Big Bend: "Don't try to conserve the drinking water you have." The chapter had said the Army says to ration (true only of Ch 4's general line). Now: do not ration, ration sweat. Fig 3 caption updated. Consistent with content/water.html ("Save sweat, not water").
3. Rule-of-3s water row: dropped Wikipedia-only items (18-day record, 7-day desert case, "hours" at high heat). Replaced with Tipton & Montgomery 2025, Experimental Physiology (doi:10.1113/EP093194): death usually after ~15-20% body-water loss, as little as 3 days in hot climates; need ~1 L/day at rest to 4 L+/day active in heat. Myth callout and Fig 1 water label/bar updated to match.
4. Food row: replaced "no study shows..." with Tipton & Montgomery (total starvation with water: death usually within 40-60 days). Fig 1 food label and bar updated (was ~1 wk-90 days). ScienceAlert source removed; 382-day fast stays on ACUTE.
5. Exposure row: "death from heat stroke within hours" was not supported by MedlinePlus. Replaced with CDC/NIOSH: body temperature can reach 106°F (41°C) or higher within 10-15 minutes; fatal if treatment is delayed.
6. Cold-water callout: clarified that the Army's 25-30x figure refers to moving in cold water (re-read army.mil page); added "often 30 to 60 minutes" to the hypothermia stage and a cross-ref to keep it consistent with environmental-emergencies.html.
7. HELP position: wording aligned with the Army page (knees pulled to chest, a floating ball); huddle: arms around shoulders (the Army wording, replacing "linked arms").
8. Sea: added the Army sea-chapter note that in a life raft you do set a daily ration based on supply and group condition, rest, and stay shaded. This is so the land "don't ration" advice is not misapplied at sea. Fig 5 caption notes that the joint manual puts water before signals.
9. Stay-or-go: the NPS "stay put" claim had no NPS source in the list. Opened the NPS Outdoor Emergency Plan PDF (confirms: stay put, moving makes you harder to find, move only on an immediate threat) and added it.
10. Sources: added Tipton & Montgomery 2025, CDC/NIOSH Heat-related Illnesses, NPS Outdoor Emergency Plan. Removed ScienceAlert. Wikipedia is now flagged "used only for the rule's history".
11. Fig 1: moved the "382 days (supervised)" label up so it does not touch the diamond.

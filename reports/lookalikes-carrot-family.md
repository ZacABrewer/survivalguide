## Could not verify / disagreements
- "Most toxic plant in North America": only USDA ARS says "most violently toxic"; Maine DACF says "one of the most toxic native"; MD Poison Center says "one of the most toxic". Chapter attributes it to USDA ARS and calls it a ranking.
- Children and whistles from hemlock stems: sourced only to an eMedicine/Medscape page (URL topic875; page title not confirmed in the fetch) plus Cornell ("children handling stems"). INCHEM did not mention whistles. Worded as "a toxicology reference reports".
- Bracts: wild carrot "ring of branched bracts" (Ontario) kept; "three-forked" not verified, so left out. Poison hemlock bract details NOT verified; figure 3 draws no bracts on hemlock and text does not claim any.
- "Oily" liquid in water hemlock root: not verified. Sources say yellowish (reddish brown in air, TAMU), brownish/straw (USDA), yellow-orange (Norway). Chapter says "yellowish liquid".
- Leaf veins to notches: confirmed by TAMU, CT CAES, USDA ARS. TAMU alone says it does not occur in other Apiaceae. Figure 5 "typical toothed leaf: veins to tips" is general botany, not directly sourced.
- Heights disagree: giant hogweed 15-20 ft (UMN) vs 12-15 ft (Poison.org) vs over 8 ft (Maine); cow parsnip 5-8 ft (Maine) vs 4-15 ft (NC State); fool's parsley 15-60 cm (Norway) vs up to 120 cm (Massey). Ranges shown with attribution.
- Fool's parsley toxicity: old literature severe; Norwegian guide says no modern cases. Described as toxic and poorly studied. Poison hemlock "mouse-like" odor from Cornell/INCHEM; unscented/other descriptors not included.
- Poison hemlock onset: ~20 min (MD Poison Center); INCHEM and Arkansas give no firm timing ("minutes" per Arkansas). Death under 3 h in severe cases (INCHEM).
- Phytophotodermatitis timing: onset within 24 h, peak by 3 days (Poison.org); blisters within 48 h (NY DOH). Cover skin: UMN at least 48 h; Poison.org 1-2 days. Dermatology Online Journal full text was inaccessible (abstract only).
- Wild parsnip root edibility: no extension source found saying it is safe; chapter does not recommend it.
- Hemlock umbel shape (looser, rounded): Illinois Extension; wild carrot flat/dense from Illinois/Iowa State.
- Cross-references to other chapters are generic (no Part numbers), because the site outline was unknown.

## Could not verify / sources disagree
- Wet/river rocks bursting: the Army manual text says wet or porous rocks may explode. The detailed mechanism (steam, quartz, shards, the list of risky rocks) is from IFLScience, which cites no official source. NPS campfire page says nothing about rocks.
- Burning poison ivy/oak/sumac: Alabama Extension states it plainly (airway swelling, can be fatal). NIOSH page said burning causes respiratory problems; the older NIOSH 2010-118 page gave no content. No second authoritative page on the lung effects.
- Oleander: only the Arizona poison center confirms smoke as an exposure route. Nothing found on burning the wood specifically. Chapter says "do not burn".
- Treated wood: CA State Parks and NPIC confirm. Neither names paint hazards in detail; "painted" is listed by CA Parks.
- Army manual was read via a GitHub mirror, not an official Army site. Mirror has an internal typo (bow diameter). Bow drill sizes disagree: Wilderness College (fireboard ~12 in x 1 in, spindle ~8 in x 1 in), Army (spindle ~3/4 in x 10 in), Lifesong (3/4 in x 9-10 in). Chapter shows the ranges.
- Bow drill woods (cedar, cottonwood, willow, basswood, aspen, yucca) confirmed by Wikipedia/Wilderness College/Lifesong, which are hobbyist or encyclopedia sources, not expert-reviewed. Lifesong did not mention aspen; Wikipedia and Wilderness College did.
- Chaga and Fomes: Fomes fomentarius (PopSci, FFSC) is solid. Chaga as spark-taking tinder is from Practical Self Reliance only. Lightly worded.
- Size ladder (pencil-lead, pencil, thumb, wrist): only "under 1 inch" kindling (REI) and "wrist" (REI, LNT, Army) are sourced. Pencil/thumb labels are the conventional teaching ladder, unsourced numerically.
- "How much to gather": no source gives a quantity; chapter says so.
- Left out for lack of verification: waterproofing matches, steel wool and battery detail (Army only covers touching battery wires), hand drill technique detail, long-fire/top-down details beyond Patriot Supply/Primal Survivor, distress-signal fire patterns.
- Fire lay uses (log cabin, star, long, top-down, Dakota) come from commercial survival blogs (Patriot Supply, Primal Survivor), not agencies.
- Some figure geometry was hand-built; worth a visual check in the rendered site (Figures 3, 6, 8).

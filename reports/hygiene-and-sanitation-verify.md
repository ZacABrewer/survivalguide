# Verification report: hygiene-and-sanitation (Part 7B Hygiene & Sanitation)

Verifier: independent fact-check, November 16, 2026. Web searches used: 4 of 10.

## Changes made
1. **Tippy-tap: added sourced construction steps and redrew Figure 2.** Source: CDC tippy-tap instructions (stacks.cdc.gov/view/cdc/41617). Nine numbered steps (outlet hole one finger below the top on the side opposite the handle, hot-nail holes, rope from cap to long foot stick, soap on short rope, fill with treated water, hang so rope lifts the stick). Old figure had the outlet near the jug bottom and a "foot string" tied to the jug base, which does not match the CDC design (a low hole would leak constantly). New figure: jug hung by handle, cap rope to a long foot stick, outlet near top, soap on string. Gravel pit is kept as unsourced common sense (not attributed). Watershed Management Group re-opened: confirms 1 gal serves 15-30 people, shared soap OK, air dry.
2. **Hantavirus/rodent cleanup (CDC clean-up page re-opened):** confirmed exactly "1.5 cups of household bleach in 1 gallon of water (or 1 part bleach to 9 parts water)", fresh each time, 30 min airing, 5 min soak or label time. Added the CDC's 1:9 ratio, approx. 350 mL, EPA-registered disinfectant option, 20-min vehicle airing, "label time" option, regularly-emptied bin, dry-on-high/sun for bedding, and fuller heavy-infestation PPE (rubber/latex/vinyl gloves, half-mask or powered respirator with HEPA filters). Figure 8 step 3 now shows "(1 part to 9 parts)".
3. **Lye first aid:** the 15-minute flush rested on BrambleBerry only, and "Do not use vinegar. It can add heat" had no source. Replaced with MedlinePlus (NLM) sodium hydroxide poisoning guidance: flush skin/eyes with lots of water at least 15 minutes; swallowed: don't induce vomiting, give water or milk only if alert and able to swallow; inhaled: fresh air; get medical help; Poison Help 1-800-222-1222 (from MedlinePlus). Vinegar line softened to "use plain water; don't waste time on neutralizers". NIOSH re-opened: confirms corrosive, incompatible with water, aluminum, tin, zinc; eye/skin irrigate immediately; swallow, seek medical attention.
4. **Repellents:** figure labels said "Strong: EPA, CDC", implying an official grade. Neither agency grades repellents. Changed to "CDC-listed. Strong/Moderate", citronella "Not CDC-listed. Weak", and added caption/text saying the grades are this guide's summary. Replaced the "no DEET hours" note with CDC Yellow Book facts: DEET efficacy peaks near 50%; any product under 10% may give only 1-2 h; higher concentration lasts longer; sweat, water, heat, rubbing shorten it; "pure" OLE essential oil not recommended. Poison center times re-opened and match the chart bars exactly (picaridin 20% 8-14 h, IR3535 20% 7-10 h, 3.8 h vs Anopheles, PMD 30% 4-6 h, citronella 4.2% 1 h; picaridin not under age 2). CDC mosquito page re-opened: six ingredients, OLE/PMD not under 3, sunscreen first, not under clothing, permethrin not on skin. EPA page: no hours given; advises choosing by label protection time.
5. **Lice source swap:** replaced a school-district-hosted copy of a CDC PDF with the CDC's own pages (lice/treatment and lice/hcp/clinical-care), which confirm every number in the chapter (permethrin 1% from 2 months, pyrethrins from age 2, chrysanthemum/ragweed allergy, retreat 9-10 days / around day 9, 8-12 h check, comb every 2-3 days for 2-3 weeks, no conditioner first, 130°F wash, 2-week bagging, combs 130°F 5-10 min, no fumigant sprays, max 2-3 treatments of one drug).
6. **Figure layout fixes:** Fig 3 "Cat hole" label was struck through by the arrow (moved left); Fig 4 "Runoff turned away" overlapped the dashed arrow (moved left). All 8 figures re-rendered and viewed; no remaining overlaps or out-of-box labels.
7. Sources list updated (+CDC tippy-tap, CDC lice x2, MedlinePlus, CDC Yellow Book, EPA repellent page; −school-hosted PDF). 30 sources.

## Claims verified
- Cat hole 6-8 in deep, 4-6 in wide, 200 ft from water/trails/camp, 70-80 paces; TP bury or pack out, don't burn; pack out wipes, pads, tampons; cup waste in cat hole; dishwater 200 ft and strain; bathing 200 ft (Leave No Trace).
- IFRC/FSM-TWiG emergency standards: 1 toilet per 20, max 50 m from dwelling, at least 30 m from water sources, pit bottom 1.5 m above water table, "increase distance" in fissured rock/limestone, soap or ash, menstrual disposal container. The chapter uses 200 ft (60 m) for all water, consistent with Part 0 › When Help Isn't Coming (Fig 7). content/water.html has no latrine distances, so there is no conflict. The "wet-season" qualifier is WEDC's (cited in the other chapter) and is the conservative reading.
- Scabies (CDC): >122°F (50°C) for 10 min, bag at least 72 h up to a week, mites survive about 2-3 days off skin, 2-month contacts, OTC products don't work, infants per pediatrician only.
- Head lice basics (CDC): crawl, can't hop or fly; head-to-head spread; no disease; nits >¼ in from scalp mean an old infestation.

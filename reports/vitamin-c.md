Words: about 3,100 prose (incl. captions), about 3,620 total with tables and sources. Figures: 6 inline SVGs (scurvy body diagram, rose hip, conifer comparison, bar chart of foods, potato-loss chart, jar-sprouting steps). Sources: 25.

Sources opened: NIH ODS (health professional and consumer fact sheets), USDA FoodData Central (values read via its API: rose hips 426, kale raw 93.4, cabbage 36.6, lentils dry 4.5, lentil sprouts 16.5, mung sprouts 13.2 and stir-fried 16.0, beef liver raw 1.3, soybeans 6.0, kimchi 0.0), StatPearls NBK493187, James Lind Library, WHO Bulletin (SciELO), Holman Inuit diet paper (Arctic), PubMed Bintje potato abstract, PubMed cooking-methods abstract, FAO AGRIS dried-vegetable storage, K-State MF1181, BASF ascorbic acid data, Ho-Chunk pine needle tea, WebMD, USDA ARS ponderosa pine, NC State yew, poison.org yew, Purdue Norfolk pine, Missouri Dept of Conservation sumac, Common Sense Home rose hips, IAS mung bean abstract, Springer mung bean light abstract, UConn sprouts, Virginia Tech and UNL sprouting guides.

Verified: scurvy onset (NIH ~1 month under ~10 mg/day; StatPearls 4-12 weeks); recovery times; UL values and side effects; Lind 1747 (HMS Salisbury, 12 men, 6 treatments; citrus group recovered best); yew toxicity, flat two-ranked needles, red aril; ponderosa pine abortion in cattle; Norfolk Island pine not a true pine; sprouting raises vitamin C; sprout food-safety rules.

Not verified / disagreements / judgment calls:
- Raw organ meats: USDA raw beef liver is only 1.3 mg/100 g, so the chapter says liver is not a vitamin C source. The Arctic paper shows ringed seal liver, muktuk, and fresh/frozen meat helped. No Arctic figure for other organs was clear (the paper's table was garbled).
- Ponderosa/lodgepole pine pregnancy caution: evidence is from cattle (USDA ARS); no human data found. The chapter says so and gives the conservative advice (Ho-Chunk guidance).
- Vitamin C content of pine needle tea and staghorn sumac: no authoritative figure found; stated as unmeasured.
- Wild greens: none verified; left out except a pointer to a forager.
- Fir needles also look flat; I did not verify a fir-vs-yew rule, so the text only says to walk away unless sure.
- Potato data is one French Bintje study abstract (1983); other sources (1935 Idaho bulletin) say storage losses were smaller. Conservative figure used.
- Rose hip prep (hairs irritate, syrup loss ~15%) comes from a hobby site (Common Sense Home), not an institution; USDA 426 mg/100 g is an SR Legacy value.
- Ascorbic acid shelf life (36 months, below 25C) is from a manufacturer data sheet.
- Several PubMed/PMC pages were blocked (reCAPTCHA), so cooking-method percentages beyond the abstract (0-91%, microwave best, boil worst) were not obtained.
- Mung bean light study (78x) and 62% rise come from abstracts only.
- 90 mg x 365 days = about 33 g is my own arithmetic.

## Disagreements and gaps
- **Cord clamp timing:** WHO says 1 to 3 minutes. StatPearls says 30 to 60 seconds, up to 3 minutes. The text says wait at least 1 minute, cites WHO, and notes ACOG could not be opened.
- **Tie distance:** WHO says 2 cm and 5 cm from the belly. StatPearls says about 10 cm, and Ohio State says leave 6 to 8 in. The text gives WHO's numbers, flags the disagreement, and says a longer stump is safer.
- **Bleeding thresholds:** CDC (one pad per hour, clots bigger than an egg), WHO guide (a pad soaked in under 5 minutes, or more than 2 to 3 pads in 20 to 30 minutes), and WHO provider guide (500 mL) differ. The text uses the lowest lay cutoff.
- **Tight nuchal cord:** WHO says clamp and cut. StatPearls calls that a last resort. The text tells lay readers not to force it and to call for help.
- **Turning interval:** Merck and MedlinePlus say every 2 hours. The old CDC-hosted manual says hourly. The text says at least every 2 hours, more often if skin stays red.
- **Cord prolapse:** the WHO guide gives a clinician maneuver (push the baby's head up). The text gives only knee-chest position and no pushing for lay readers, and keeps the clinician step for trained providers.

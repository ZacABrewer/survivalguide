## Disagreements and weak spots
- Canned-food life: NDSU gives high-acid up to 18 months, low-acid 2-5 years; UGA says best within 1 year, rotate every 2-4 years. Chapter says to use the shorter time.
- Baking powder: UGA lists it as keeping indefinitely, NDSU says 6 months unopened. Mentioned in text; shorter advised.
- Candles: Red Cross winter page says avoid candles; Michigan lists a candle in the car kit. Chapter gives the conservative battery-light advice.
- 3-month and 1-year tiers: no agency defines 3-month or 1-year stockpiles; stated in the text as the author's own tiers. No calorie or bulk-quantity figures were given (UGA's old per-adult-per-year table was not used because it is a single older source).
- Fuel limits: only a single municipal page (Lehi, UT) was opened. Presented as an example, with "check your local fire department." NFPA/EPA pages could not be opened. No gasoline shelf-life number was given.
- Sleeping bag ISO 23537 and ASTM F3340 details come from secondary sources (Australian Hiker, Section Hiker, REI), flagged as such. The ISO standard text itself was not opened. REI's "10 to 15 F buffer" is used for the bag-selection example. R-value table is REI's. The REI fetch calls the standard "ASTM FF3340" in places (typo in source); used F3340.
- Engine idle rule (5 to 10 minutes per hour) rests on the Michigan page only; attributed to them by name.
- Unsourced general-practice statements kept qualitative: saw suggestions (more control, less room than an axe), headlamp hands-free, one battery size across kits, knife/tool/bag care steps in the maintenance table, "get-home" and "bug-out" definitions (community terms; stated so in text). Water weight "about 8 lb per gallon (3.6 kg)" is physical common knowledge, not from an opened page.
- Hot-car figures: NWS Houston page (opened) and NHTSA release both agree on ~20F in 10 minutes.

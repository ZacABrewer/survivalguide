## Changes made (reason, source)

### Boiling
- **Step 3 and the "sources differ" paragraph rewritten.**
  - Both agencies are now stated exactly. CDC: 1 minute, or 3 minutes above 6,500 ft (CDC hiking page; CDC Make Water Safe page). EPA: at least 1 minute, or 3 minutes above 5,000 ft (EPA page updated Feb 24, 2026).
  - The EPA's own metric figure is "1,000 meters", which does not match 5,000 ft. The chapter now says so and gives 5,000 ft as about 1,500 m.
  - The chapter credits its choice of 3 minutes above 5,000 ft to the EPA as the more cautious agency.
  - Dropped the Colorado State "2,000 m" figure. It equals the CDC cutoff and wrongly suggested a range.
  - Army rule completed from FM 21-76: 1 minute plus 1 minute per 300 m (about 1,000 ft), or 10 minutes anywhere.
- **Figure 5 redrawn.** The 3-minute band started at 6,500 ft, which contradicted the text. It now starts at 5,000 ft. EPA and CDC markers kept. Caption rewritten and the "5,000 ft (1,000 m)" error removed.

### Bleach
- **Table matches EPA exactly.** Rows: 2/2 drops per quart; 8/6 per gallon; 16 (1/4 tsp) / 12 (1/8 tsp) for 2 gallons; 1/3 / 1/4 tsp for 4 gallons; 2/3 / 1/2 tsp for 8 gallons. The rules also match: double for cloudy, colored or very cold water; 30 minutes; slight chlorine smell, otherwise repeat and wait 15 minutes; unscented only; fresh bleach or under a year at room temperature.
- **Ready.gov sentence fixed.** It said Ready.gov's dose "is the 6% column". Ready.gov actually gives 1/8 teaspoon per gallon for 5.25–6% bleach.
- **Added the CDC's simpler rule:** 8 drops per gallon for any 5–9% bleach (CDC Make Water Safe). The "throw it out if still no smell" step stays, sourced to Ready.gov.

### Iodine and chlorine dioxide
- **Iodine:** added "or colored" to the 10-drop case (EPA). The dose and the CDC cautions (pregnancy, thyroid, iodine allergy, no more than a few weeks) were already correct.
- **Chlorine dioxide rewritten.**
  - The paragraph now leads with the CDC: "somewhat effective" against *Cryptosporidium* (CDC hiking page and backcountry table), needs "several hours" of contact (CDC Yellow Book 2026), follow the label (CDC).
  - The 4-hour figure is kept but named as Colorado State University Extension's. I re-opened that page and it says 4 hours. I found no CDC or EPA figure in hours.

### Filters
- **"absolute" added** to the 1-micron and 0.3-micron lines, to match the CDC wording.
- **0.3 micron kept.** The current CDC page and backcountry table say 0.3, not 0.1. The CDC Yellow Book gives 0.2–0.4 µm for bacteria.
- **Extension sentence replaced.** It gave a confusing "<0.1 micron for protozoa" figure, and the extension page contradicts itself on this. It is replaced with the Yellow Book point that only ultrafilters (about 0.01 µm) or reverse osmosis can be trusted against viruses.
- **Carbon-filter claim removed.** "Carbon filters are not rated for germs" was unsourced and too broad. It now reads "a filter sold only to improve taste does not make water safe".

### SODIS
- **Table row changed** from "Weak / Weak / Yes / Not reliable" to "Partial" in all four germ columns. Both CDC Yellow Books (2024 and 2026) say only that SODIS "can improve" water quality. Neither rates it germ by germ, and the CDC backcountry table leaves it out.
- **Callout rewritten** to say that sources differ:
  - CDC: improves quality only.
  - Akvopedia: weaker results against viruses and protozoa than bacteria.
  - The PMC study: it used 25 L jerrycans and 2 L bottles, and only cites other work on viruses. The old wording wrongly made this a finding about bottles.
- **Duplicate CDC timing sentence trimmed** from the intro.
- **Removed "Cool the water before drinking"**, which had no source.
- **Yellow Book citation updated** to the 2026 edition. The 2024 edition stays listed.

### Storage
- **Moved from the archived 2012 CDC page to the current CDC page** (June 27, 2025): 1 gallon per person per day for 3 days, a 2-week supply if possible, store more for hot climates, pregnancy, illness and pets, 50–70°F, replace every 6 months, and no glass or containers that held chemicals (bleach named).
- **Ready.gov confirmed:** 1 gallon per person per day "for several days", replace every 6 months. Ready.gov does not mention 2 weeks.
- **Treating bullet fixed.** "1/8 tsp (about 8 drops)" mixed two agencies' conversions. It now points to the EPA table: 8 drops of 6% bleach per gallon, 30 minutes.
- **Rinse vs. air-dry** credited to Ready.gov and CDC respectively.
- **Milk/juice jug warning:** kept, sourced to the archived CDC page, which stays in the list.

## Claims verified (brief)
- **CDC (Find Clean Water page):** urban hidden sources (water heater tank, not the heating-system tank; toilet tank only if clear and untreated; ice; canned liquid; pools for washing only); fuel or toxic chemicals mean the water cannot be made safe; follow officials on the main valve.
- **CDC (rainwater page):** lead, copper and asbestos; first flush; do not mix rainwater with treated water.
- **Ready.gov:**
  - Needs: at least 1 gallon per person per day; "in very hot temperatures, water needs can double"; children, nursing mothers and sick people may need more.
  - Distillation steps and the 20-minute boil.
  - Rejection of iodine.
- **Bleach and iodine doses:** as listed above (EPA).
- **Army FM 21-76:** 2–3 L at 20°C; 14–30 L in an extreme arid climate; hourly pacing; "do not ration"; don't eat when water is short; bamboo, vines (sticky, milky or bitter) and banana stumps (first three fillings bitter); filtering does not purify; charcoal for odor.
- **Multi-Service manual:** old vs. new sea ice; liquids not to drink (urine, blood, seawater, alcohol, melt from new sea ice); eating snow lowers body temperature and injures lips and mouth; pale yellow urine; poisonous plants give poisonous liquid; water tastes like the plant.
- **Phoenix Fire:** sit 12 in off the ground; dig 3–6 ft at the outside of a bend.
- **NPS Big Bend:** 1 gallon per day; don't ration.
- **MedlinePlus:** dehydration signs.
- **SODIS:** 6 hours, or 2 days when cloudy (CDC Yellow Book 2026). PET only, under 30 NTU, three-quarters fill and 20-second shake, not in continuous rain, reflective surface (Akvopedia).
- **UV:** kills bacteria, viruses, *Giardia* and *Cryptosporidium* in clear water (Yellow Book 2026; CDC backcountry table).
- **Cactus:** Britannica.
- **Seawater:** NOAA.
